# Niche Scripts: Penryn & Falmouth

Tailored openers, discovery questions and pitches for each type of business. Use these inside the master framework (`00-master-cold-call-script.md`): pattern interrupt, then permission, then reason, then discovery, then pitch, then close.

Each niche has:
- **The pain point:** what they're actually losing by not having a site
- **Prep before you dial:** what to check in 2 minutes
- **Opener:** pattern interrupt plus permission
- **Discovery questions:** pick 2–3
- **Pitch:** tie it back to what they told you
- **What their site needs:** so you sound like you know their world
- **Close:** the easiest next step for this niche

Placeholders: `[YOUR NAME]`, `[OWNER NAME]`, `[BUSINESS]`, `[PRICE]`, `[AREA]`, `[TIMEFRAME]`.

> **A note on claims:** where a pitch mentions how customers search, it's based on the BrightLocal survey figures cited at the bottom.[^1] Don't add your own numbers unless you can source them. Phrases like "a lot of people" or "most people I speak to" are fine and honest.

## 1. Trades: plumbers, electricians, builders, roofers

**The pain point:** they're good and busy, but the **higher-value jobs** (bathroom refits, rewires, extensions, re-roofs) increasingly go to whoever looks most credible online. Homeowners, especially second-home owners and people who've just moved to Falmouth, don't have a local network to ask. They search "roofer Falmouth" or go on Checkatrade or MyBuilder, and competitors who've paid for those listings or have a proper site get the call. The trades person ends up with the small, fiddly jobs from word of mouth and misses the big quotes.

**Prep:** check whether they're on Checkatrade, TrustATrader or MyBuilder, look at their Google reviews, and note their van livery if you've seen it. Do they show a Gas Safe or NICEIC number anywhere?

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I'll be quick, I know you're probably about to head to a job. I've seen your van about [Penryn/Falmouth], and I tried to look you up online and could only find [your Facebook / a Yell listing]. I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me if it's worth chatting?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "Are you as busy as you want to be, or is it more that you're busy with the wrong sort of jobs?"
2. "When someone's after a bigger job, like a new bathroom or a re-roof, how do they usually find you?"
3. "Do you ever lose quotes to the Checkatrade lot, or people who come up first on Google?"
4. "Do you get many time-wasters, people ringing for a price on something you don't even do?"
5. "Is there a part of the area you'd rather work more in, like Falmouth town or out towards Mylor and Constantine, to cut down driving?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said you're losing some of the bigger jobs to people on Checkatrade. Those sites charge a monthly fee and put you right next to five competitors on the same page. Your own site is yours. It shows photos of your actual work, your reviews, your Gas Safe/NICEIC number, and the areas you cover. So when a homeowner in Falmouth searches for a [roofer], they find you and they can see you're the real deal before they ring. It also filters people: if your site says 'we specialise in bathroom refits across Falmouth and Penryn', you get more of those calls and fewer 'can you change a tap washer in Truro' ones."

**What their site needs:** gallery of before/after work, services list, area covered, accreditations (Gas Safe, NICEIC, NAPIT, FMB, guarantees), Google reviews, click-to-call button, a simple "request a quote" form with photo upload.

**Close:**
> "I know evenings are your admin time, so how about I put together a quick mockup with a few of your job photos off Facebook, send it by text, and you can have a look when you're home? Then a five-minute call if you like it."

## 2. Cafés, restaurants & takeaways

**The pain point:** **tourists and day-trippers in summer** are standing on Church Street or by Events Square with their phones, searching "café near me Falmouth", "best fish and chips Falmouth" or "breakfast Penryn". They decide fast, and they want a menu, opening hours, whether there's vegan or gluten-free, and whether dogs are allowed. A Facebook page that's hard to find, or that shows last March's hours, loses them to the place two doors down. In winter, the same site helps locals and students find specials, events and takeaway options.

**Prep:** check their Google listing hours (are they right?), Tripadvisor rating, whether there's a menu anywhere online, and whether they're on Just Eat, Deliveroo or Uber Eats, and so paying commission.

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm local here in Falmouth. I'll be honest, it's a cold call, and I've deliberately waited till after the lunch rush! I was trying to find your menu online the other day and couldn't, only [your Facebook / Google photos]. Can I have 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and you can tell me if it's worth a chat?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "How do most of your summer customers find you, mainly walk-ins, or do people come looking for you specifically?"
2. "Do you get many calls or messages asking 'what's on the menu', 'are you open', 'are dogs allowed'?"
3. "What's winter like for you, and is there anything you'd love to fill more, like quiet weekdays or evenings?"
4. "Do you do takeaway or delivery? Are you using Just Eat or Deliveroo, and how do you feel about the commission?"
5. "Do you ever do events, like live music, supper clubs, Christmas bookings?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said summer's walk-ins and winter's quiet. In summer, visitors don't know anyone here, so they just search 'café near me' on their phone and pick from the first few they see, and they want to see the menu and hours before they walk over. A simple site with your menu, photos, opening times and 'dogs welcome' right there means you're the one they pick. In winter it's where you put your specials, Christmas bookings and events, so locals and students have a reason to come in. [If they use delivery apps:] And if you wanted, we could add a simple 'order for collection' page so some of those orders come straight to you without the commission."

**What their site needs:** menu (easy to update, or a PDF-free web menu), opening hours that match Google, photos of the food and space, dietary info, dog-friendly/accessibility notes, map and directions, table booking or click-to-call, collection ordering if relevant, Christmas/events page.

**Close:**
> "How about this: I mock up a homepage with your menu and a few of your Instagram/Facebook photos and send it over. You can look at it on your phone between services. If you like it, we have a 15-minute chat on your quiet day."

## 3. Hair, beauty & barbers

**The pain point:** **bookings and no-shows**. Clients message on Instagram or Facebook at 11pm asking "have you got anything Saturday?", and the owner answers between clients or loses the booking. New people in town (students starting at Falmouth University or the Penryn Campus each September, people moving to the area, wedding guests in summer) search "barber Falmouth" or "nails Penryn" and want to see prices, photos and **book instantly**. Without a site and online booking, they go elsewhere.

**Prep:** check whether they use a booking app already (Fresha, Booksy, Treatwell, Timely) and whether there's a price list anywhere online. Look at their Instagram for good photos.

**Opener:**
> "Hiya, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a local web designer in [Penryn/Falmouth]. I know you're probably between clients, so I'll be quick. I was looking at your Instagram, your work's lovely by the way, but I couldn't find a website or price list. I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to say why, and you can tell me if it's worth chatting?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "How do people book with you at the moment: phone, DMs, walk-ins?"
2. "How much time do you spend replying to messages asking prices or availability?"
3. "Do you get no-shows, and do you take deposits?"
4. "Do you notice a bump when the students come back in September, or with weddings in the summer?"
5. "Are you using something like Fresha or Booksy already?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said you're answering DMs about prices and availability in the evenings. A site with your price list, your best photos and an 'Book now' button means people can see everything and book themselves at midnight if they want, without you lifting a finger. [If they use Fresha/Booksy:] And it'd plug straight into your Fresha, so nothing changes in how you work, it just sends more people there. When new students arrive in Penryn in September searching 'barber near me', you're the one with prices and a booking button right there."

**What their site needs:** price list, gallery (link Instagram feed), online booking link or embed, deposit/cancellation policy, meet-the-team, opening hours, location/parking, gift vouchers (big at Christmas), bridal/wedding page for beauty salons.

**Close:**
> "Tell you what, I'll put together a quick mockup using a few of your Instagram photos and your prices, and send it on WhatsApp. Have a look when you've got a gap. If you like it, we'll grab 15 minutes on your quiet day."

## 4. B&Bs, guest houses & holiday lets

**The pain point:** **commission**. Most rely on Booking.com, Airbnb or agencies (Classic Cottages, Cornish Gems, Sykes, etc.), which take a significant cut of every booking. Repeat guests and people who find them by name would happily book direct, but there's nowhere to send them. They also lose late bookings around **Falmouth Week (August)**, sailing events, graduation weekends at Falmouth University and Exeter's Penryn Campus, and weddings, because guests can't check availability quickly.

**Prep:** find them on Booking.com/Airbnb, note their rating and roughly what they charge per night. Check for a "book direct" option. Are there lots of returning-guest reviews?

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I'll be straight with you, it's a cold call, but I've waited till after breakfast service! I found [BUSINESS] on [Booking.com/Airbnb], lovely reviews, but couldn't find a way to book with you direct. I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me if it's worth a chat?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "Roughly what share of your bookings come through Booking.com or Airbnb versus direct?"
2. "Do you get many repeat guests? How do they book with you again?"
3. "Do you get enquiries for Falmouth Week, graduations or weddings? How do you handle those?"
4. "What are your shoulder seasons like, spring and autumn? Anything you'd love to fill?"
5. "Do you get the same questions every time: parking, dogs, how far to Gyllyngvase beach?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said about [X]% comes through Booking.com. Every one of those costs you commission. Your returning guests already love you, and you've got reviews saying so. A site of your own gives them, and anyone who Googles you by name, a way to book direct, so you keep the full amount. It doesn't replace Booking.com, it just means you're not paying them for guests who'd have come to you anyway. You can also put your Falmouth Week and graduation availability on it, plus the stuff people always ask: parking, dogs, walk to Gylly, ferry to St Mawes."

**What their site needs:** gallery of rooms, availability calendar or booking widget (or simple enquiry form to start), rates, location and things to do nearby, FAQs (parking, dogs, check-in), guest reviews, direct-booking incentive (e.g. "book direct for the best rate"), events page (Falmouth Week, Oyster Festival, graduations).

**Close:**
> "Autumn's a good time to get this sorted before next season's bookings start coming in. How about I mock up a homepage using your photos, so you can see what it'd look like, and then we chat for 15 minutes about whether direct booking would work for you?"

> **Check before you say it:** commission rates vary by platform and agreement. Ask them what they pay rather than quoting a percentage.

## 5. Retail & independent shops

**The pain point:** Falmouth and Penryn have strong independent high streets, but shops depend on footfall, which drops hard in winter. Visitors who loved the shop in summer go home and **can't find them again** to buy online or check what's new. Locals searching "gifts Falmouth" or "independent bookshop near me" in December find the chains first. Without a site, they can't capture Christmas, gift vouchers, or "click and collect".

**Prep:** check their Instagram (lots of indies sell via DMs), whether they have an Etsy shop, and their Google hours. Look at what they stock: local makers, art, homeware, clothing, surf gear?

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm local, in [Falmouth/Penryn]. I'll be honest, this is a cold call, but I'm a big fan of the shop, I've been in a few times. I noticed you haven't got a website, just [Instagram/Facebook]. Can I have 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and you can tell me if it's worth a chat?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "How big is the difference between summer and winter for you?"
2. "Do visitors ever message you after they've gone home, wanting to buy something they saw?"
3. "Do you sell anything online at the moment, through Instagram DMs, Etsy, anything like that?"
4. "How important is Christmas for you? Do you do gift vouchers?"
5. "Do you stock local makers? Is that a big part of what people come in for?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said winter's tough and visitors sometimes message after they've gone home. A site gives those people somewhere to come back to: a small online shop with your bestsellers, gift vouchers, and 'click and collect' for locals. It doesn't need to be a huge online store. Even 20 of your best products and a gift voucher page could carry you through January. And when someone in Falmouth searches 'gifts near me' in December, you're there with your opening hours and what's in stock."

**What their site needs:** small product catalogue or full shop (Shopify/WooCommerce/Square depending on budget), gift vouchers, click-and-collect, opening hours, "our makers" page, newsletter sign-up, Instagram feed, Christmas opening times.

**Close:**
> "Christmas is the obvious one. If we started in the next couple of weeks, you'd have gift vouchers online in time for it. Shall I mock up a homepage with a few of your products so you can see it, then we chat for 15 minutes?"

> **Urgency check:** only use the Christmas line if the timeline genuinely works. See `02-offer-and-pricing-talk-tracks.md`.

## 6. Marine & boat services (Falmouth harbour)

**The pain point:** Falmouth has one of the world's great natural harbours, with marinas such as Port Pendennis, Falmouth Haven, Falmouth Marina and Mylor Yacht Harbour, plus boatyards along the Penryn River and at Ponsharden. Visiting yachts, new boat owners, and people chartering for summer are often from **out of the area**. They search "boat engine repair Falmouth", "rigger Falmouth", "boat trips Falmouth" or "sailing lessons Falmouth" on their phones. Marine businesses run heavily on word of mouth along the pontoons, but **visiting sailors and newcomers have no one to ask**. Tour and charter businesses lose bookings to whoever has online booking.

**Prep:** check whether they're listed by the marina or harbour, what services they offer (engineering, rigging, sail repair, valeting, moorings, charters, boat trips, RYA courses), and whether they have reviews.

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I'll be honest, it's a cold call, but I've waited for a grotty day so hopefully you're not out on the water! I was looking up marine [engineers/riggers/charters] round the harbour and couldn't find a website for [BUSINESS]. Can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me if it's worth chatting?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "How do visiting boats find you when they come into Falmouth needing work?"
2. "Is most of your work from regulars on the moorings, or do you get a lot of visitors too?"
3. "How busy do you get around Falmouth Week and the regatta season? Do you ever have to turn work away, or is it more that people can't find you?"
4. "[Charters/trips/courses:] How do people book at the moment? Do you lose bookings from people who can't get through on the phone?"
5. "What's the winter like? Lay-ups, refits, antifouling?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said visiting boats tend to find you through the marina office or by luck. A visiting sailor with an engine problem is on their phone searching 'marine engineer Falmouth' and they'll ring whoever comes up with a clear site saying what they fix and how to reach them. A simple site with your services, the makes you work on, where you're based and a big 'call now' button means they ring you. [For winter:] And you can put your winter lay-up and refit packages on it, so owners book you in October instead of ringing round. [Charters:] For trips and charters, online booking means you take bookings at 10pm while you're asleep, not just when you can answer the phone on deck."

**What their site needs:** services and engine makes/specialities, where they're based (marina/yard and VHF channel if relevant), emergency/call-out info, winter lay-up/refit packages, accreditations (RYA, BMEA etc.), online booking for charters/trips/courses, weather/cancellation policy, photos.

**Close:**
> "Winter's when you've got time to think about this, and when owners are planning work for next season. How about I mock up a homepage and send it over, and if you like it we chat for 15 minutes, or I can come down to the yard."

## 7. Cleaners, gardeners & dog groomers

**The pain point:** small service businesses (often one person or a couple) with a full diary from word of mouth, until a few regulars move away and there's a gap. They rely on Facebook community groups ("Falmouth Noticeboard"-type groups, Penryn community pages) and Nextdoor, but those posts disappear in a day. **Holiday-let changeover cleaning** is a big local market, and letting agents and second-home owners search online. New residents search "gardener Falmouth" or "dog groomer near me". Without a site, they can't easily show prices, areas covered or reviews, and they spend hours answering the same messages.

**Prep:** check their Facebook for reviews and recent posts, and note what they offer (domestic, end-of-tenancy, holiday-let changeovers, garden maintenance, hedge cutting, full groom, puppy trims, mobile grooming?).

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm local here in [Penryn/Falmouth]. I'll be quick, I know you're probably out and about. I've seen your posts on [Facebook group], and you've got some lovely reviews, but I couldn't find a website. I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me if it's worth chatting?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "Is your diary full at the moment, or have you got room for a few more regulars?"
2. "When you've had gaps before, how did you fill them, posting in the Facebook groups?"
3. "[Cleaners:] Do you do holiday-let changeovers? Would you like more of those?"
4. "Do you spend much time replying to messages asking prices or whether you cover [AREA]?"
5. "[Groomers:] Do you take bookings online, or is it all phone and messages?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said you fill gaps by posting in the Facebook groups. That works, but a post's gone in a day. A simple site works all the time. Someone new to Falmouth searches 'gardener near me', finds you, sees your prices, the areas you cover and your reviews, and messages you ready to book. [Cleaners:] And holiday-let owners and agents especially look for someone they can check out online first. A page saying 'reliable changeover cleaning in Falmouth and Penryn' with reviews could bring in a steady block of summer work. [Groomers:] And an online booking button means fewer messages while you've got your hands full of wet spaniel."

**What their site needs:** services and price guide, areas covered (Falmouth, Penryn, Mylor, Flushing, Mawnan Smith, Budock etc.), reviews, booking or enquiry form, photos (before/after gardens, groomed dogs), insurance/DBS checks for cleaners, holiday-let changeover page.

**Close:**
> "Keep it simple. I'll do you a quick mockup of a one-page site with your prices and reviews and WhatsApp it over. If you like it, a 10-minute call. That's it."

**Pricing note:** these businesses are often price-sensitive. Lead with your entry package (see `02-offer-and-pricing-talk-tracks.md`).

## 8. Garages & mechanics

**The pain point:** **MOT reminders and trust**. People choosing a garage are nervous about being ripped off. They search "MOT Falmouth" or "garage near me Penryn", look at reviews and want prices up front. Students and newcomers with older cars, and visitors who break down in summer, have no local recommendation. Garages without a site lose them to chains and to garages that show MOT prices and online booking. Many independents also do a lot of repeat MOT and servicing work that could be booked online.

**Prep:** check whether they're a DVSA-approved MOT test centre, their Google reviews, and whether they specialise (4x4, classic cars, diagnostics, tyres, Japanese imports, campervans).

**Opener:**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I'll be quick, I know you've probably got a car on the ramp. I was looking up garages for MOTs round Penryn and Falmouth, and you've got cracking reviews on Google, but no website. I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me if it's worth chatting?"

**Discovery questions:**
1. "How do most new customers find you, word of mouth or Google?"
2. "How do people book MOTs: phone, walk in?"
3. "Do you get a lot of calls just asking 'how much is an MOT' or 'can you fit me in this week'?"
4. "Is there any type of work you'd like more of, like servicing, diagnostics, campervans?"
5. "Do you get visitors who've broken down in summer?"

**Pitch:**
> "You said most of your calls are people asking MOT prices or availability. A site with your MOT price, servicing prices and a 'book your MOT' form means those people just book, and you're not stopping work to answer the phone. You've already got great reviews, so we'd put those front and centre. People choosing a garage want to know they can trust you, and reviews plus clear prices do that before they ring. [If they have a speciality:] And a page for your [campervan/4x4] work would help you come up when people search for exactly that."

**What their site needs:** MOT and service prices, online booking form, list of services, makes or specialisms, reviews, DVSA approved test centre info, opening hours, location/parking, courtesy car or collection info, breakdown/recovery number if relevant.

**Close:**
> "How about I mock up a homepage with your prices and reviews, and pop in one morning when it's quiet, 10 minutes, to show you on my phone?"

## Quick reference: niche pain points

| Niche | Core pain point | Key search they're missing | Best hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trades | Losing bigger quotes to Checkatrade/online competitors | "roofer Falmouth", "electrician near me" | Credibility + photos of real work |
| Cafés / restaurants / takeaways | Summer tourists can't find menu/hours | "café near me Falmouth" | Menu, hours and "dogs welcome" on a phone |
| Hair / beauty / barbers | Answering DMs; newcomers can't book | "barber Falmouth", "nails Penryn" | Prices + book-now button |
| B&Bs / holiday lets | Commission on every booking | "[B&B NAME] Falmouth" | Direct bookings from repeat guests |
| Retail / indies | Winter footfall drop; visitors can't find them again | "gifts Falmouth" | Gift vouchers, small online shop |
| Marine / boat | Visiting sailors have no one to ask | "marine engineer Falmouth" | Services, location, call now |
| Cleaners / gardeners / groomers | Facebook posts disappear; gaps in diary | "gardener near me" | Always-on presence, holiday-let work |
| Garages / mechanics | Trust and constant price calls | "MOT Falmouth" | Prices + reviews + online booking |
