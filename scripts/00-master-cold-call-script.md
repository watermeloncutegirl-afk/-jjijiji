# Master Cold-Call Script: Websites for Penryn & Falmouth Businesses

The core framework. Niche-specific openers and pitches are in `01-niche-scripts.md`, and the offer and pricing wording is in `02-offer-and-pricing-talk-tracks.md`.

**Who you're calling:** small local businesses in Penryn, Falmouth and the surrounding area (Mylor, Flushing, Constantine, Mawnan Smith, Budock, Ponsanooth) that have **no website** and rely on Facebook, word of mouth, or a Yell/Google listing.

**What you want from the call:** a yes to a 15-minute chat, a short visit, or permission to send a free homepage mockup. You are **not** trying to sell a website on the phone.

**Tone:** friendly, relaxed, one local talking to another. No hard sell. Talk like you'd chat at the counter in their shop. If it starts to feel like a sales call, slow down and ask a question.

---

## The call at a glance

| Stage | Goal | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pattern-interrupt opener | Get past the "oh, a sales call" reflex | 5 sec |
| 2. Permission-based opener | Ask for 30 seconds and give them control | 10 sec |
| 3. Reason for the call | Say clearly why them, why now | 20 sec |
| 4. Discovery (2–3 questions) | Find their pain in their own words | 1–3 min |
| 5. Value pitch | Tie a website to what they just told you | 30–60 sec |
| 6. Close | 15-min chat, visit, or free mockup | 30 sec |

---

## 1. Pattern-interrupt opener

People expect a script, so open with something honest or a bit unusual. Pick one and make it your own.

**Option A: honest and light**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? Hiya, it's [YOUR NAME]. I'll be honest with you, this is a cold call, so feel free to hang up on me… but I'm local, I'm in [Falmouth/Penryn], and it's about [BUSINESS] specifically."

**Option B: local hook**
> "Hi [OWNER NAME], it's [YOUR NAME]. I was walking down [Arwenack Street / Market Street / Commercial Road / Lower Market Street in Penryn] the other day and saw your place. Have you got a sec? I won't keep you."

**Option C: the "I looked you up" interrupt**
> "Hi, is that [OWNER NAME]? It's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I tried to find [BUSINESS] online last week and couldn't find a website, only your [Facebook page / Google listing]. Is that right, or have I missed it?"

> Option C works well because it's a genuine question. They'll usually say "no, we haven't got one", and you're straight into a conversation.

**Why it works:** saying "this is a cold call" out loud removes the tension. Being local and naming a real street makes you a neighbour, not a call centre.

---

## 2. Permission-based opener

Straight after the interrupt, ask for permission and give them a way out:

> "Look, I'm calling out of the blue, so can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and then you can tell me if it's worth chatting? If it's not, no worries at all."

If they say **"go on then"**, move to stage 3.
If they say **"I'm busy"**, use this:
> "Totally understand, I've caught you mid-job. When's better, later today or tomorrow morning?" *(Book a time and actually call back then.)*

If they say **"not interested"**, ask once and keep it light:
> "Fair enough. Can I ask, is that because you've got the website side sorted, or just that it's not a priority right now?" *(If they still say no, thank them and end the call warmly. See the do/don't list.)*

---

## 3. Reason for the call

Keep it short and specific to them:

> "So, I build websites for small businesses round here, mostly people who've been doing fine on word of mouth and Facebook. I noticed [BUSINESS] doesn't have its own site, and a lot of people now go on Google first before they ring anyone or pop in. I just wanted to ask a couple of quick questions to see if it's even relevant to you. Is that alright?"

**Optional grounded line** (use sparingly, never as a lecture):
> "There's a survey from BrightLocal that found most people have searched for a local business online in the last few months, and about three-quarters of local searches start on a phone.[^1] So it's the person standing on the Moor with their phone out deciding where to go."

---

## 4. Discovery questions (pick 2–3)

Ask, then **listen**. Write down their exact words, because you'll use them in the pitch.

1. **"Where do most of your new customers come from at the moment?"**
   *(Word of mouth / Facebook / passing trade / Yell. This tells you what they rely on.)*
2. **"When someone hears about you and wants to check you out, where do you send them?"**
   *(Usually "Facebook", which opens the "not everyone's on Facebook" point.)*
3. **"Is there a time of year you're quieter, or busier than you can handle?"**
   *(The seasonal Cornwall answer. It sets up summer, Falmouth Week and term-time.)*
4. **"Do you ever get asked the same questions over and over, like prices, opening times, whether you cover [AREA]?"**
   *(A website answers them for you.)*
5. **"Have you ever thought about a website and put it off? What stopped you?"**
   *(Usually cost, time, or a bad experience. Now you know the real objection.)*
6. **"If you had, say, five more of your best type of job or customer a month, would that make a difference?"**
   *(Gets them to picture the upside in their own terms.)*

**Tip:** if they talk, let them. A good discovery section is 70% them talking.

---

## 5. Value pitch, tied to their answers

Use what they said and feed it back. Formula:

> **"You mentioned [THEIR PAIN]. What a simple site would do is [SPECIFIC FIX], so [OUTCOME THEY CARE ABOUT]."**

**Examples:**

- *They rely on word of mouth:*
  > "You said most of your work's word of mouth, which is brilliant, it means you're good. The thing is, when someone gets recommended to you, they'll often Google you before they ring. If all they find is a Facebook page from 2021, some of them go with the next name down. A simple site just means the recommendation actually turns into a phone call."

- *They send people to Facebook:*
  > "Facebook's great for people who already follow you. But not everyone's on it, and loads of people won't click a Facebook link at all. A site gives you one proper address you can put on your van, your cards and your Google listing, and it works for everyone."

- *Seasonal (summer rush / quiet winter):*
  > "You said summer's mad and winter's quiet. Visitors don't know anyone to ask for recommendations. They just search 'near me' on their phone. A website helps you get found by them in summer and fills more of the gaps in winter with locals."

- *Same questions over and over:*
  > "If you're answering 'what are your prices, do you cover Mylor' five times a day, a site with that stuff on it saves you those calls, and the calls you do get are people ready to book."

Then add the **offer in one breath** (details in `02-offer-and-pricing-talk-tracks.md`):
> "I keep it simple: a clean site that looks good on a phone, with your [services/menu/prices] and a big 'call' or 'book' button, done in [TIMEFRAME] from about [PRICE]. And I'm local, so if you need anything changed you can just ring me, not a helpdesk."

---

## 6. Close (low commitment, three options)

Offer an easy next step and let them choose:

> "I'm not going to try and sell you anything on the phone. What I'd suggest is one of three things, whichever's easiest:
> 1. a quick 15-minute chat this week, by phone or over a coffee in [Falmouth/Penryn],
> 2. I pop in when you're quiet and show you a couple of sites I've done locally, or
> 3. I put together a free mockup of what your homepage could look like, no obligation, and send it over.
> Which of those sounds alright?"

**If they pick the mockup:**
> "Lovely. What's the best number or email to send it to? And is there anything you'd definitely want on it, like your prices, photos of your work, or a booking button?" *(Then confirm a time to follow up: "I'll send it by [DAY] and give you a ring [DAY] to see what you think, does that work?")*

**If they pick a chat or visit:** lock in a day and time before you hang up, then send the confirmation text (template below).

**If you already made a mockup** (see `02-offer-and-pricing-talk-tracks.md`):
> "Actually, I'll be honest, I already put together a rough draft of a homepage for [BUSINESS], just to show you rather than tell you. Can I text you the link? Takes 30 seconds to look at."

## Gatekeeper handling

In small local businesses the "gatekeeper" is usually a partner, a family member, a staff member on the till, or a receptionist at a salon or garage. Be friendly and treat them as an ally, not an obstacle.

**Opening line:**
> "Hiya, it's [YOUR NAME], I'm local here in Falmouth. Is [OWNER NAME] about? Or are you the best person to speak to about the business's online side, Google and that?"

**If you don't know the owner's name:**
> "I'm hoping you can help me. Who's the best person to speak to about how new customers find [BUSINESS]? Is that the owner?"

**"What's it regarding?"**
> "It's about getting [BUSINESS] a website. I'm a local web designer and noticed you haven't got one. It's not urgent, I just wanted a quick word with whoever looks after that."

**"They're busy / not in":**
> "No problem at all. When's usually a quieter time to catch them? …Lovely. And can I take their name, so I'm not asking for 'the owner' next time?"

**"Just send an email":**
> "Course. What's the best address? And I'll mention I spoke to you, what's your name? Thanks [NAME]." *(Send the email the same day and reference them by name.)*

**Win them over:** gatekeepers often feel the pain first (answering the phone, repeating opening times). Ask:
> "Do you get a lot of calls just asking opening times or prices?"
If yes, they can become your internal champion.

## Voicemail script (under 25 seconds)

> "Hi [OWNER NAME], it's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in [Falmouth/Penryn]. I was looking up [BUSINESS] and noticed you haven't got a website, only your [Facebook/Google listing]. I've had an idea for something simple that could bring you a few more [enquiries/bookings]. No pressure at all. My number's [YOUR NUMBER], that's [YOUR NUMBER] again. I'll also drop you a quick text so you've got my details. Cheers, bye."

**Rules:**
- Say your number twice, slowly.
- One voicemail per contact attempt, and never more than two in total.
- Always follow it with the SMS/WhatsApp below within a few minutes.

## Follow-up templates

### SMS / WhatsApp (after a voicemail or no answer)

> Hi [OWNER NAME], it's [YOUR NAME], local web designer in [Falmouth/Penryn]. Just tried to call. I noticed [BUSINESS] hasn't got a website yet and I've got a simple idea that could help you pick up more [enquiries/bookings], especially from people searching on their phones. Happy to send a free mockup of a homepage, no strings. Fancy a look? [YOUR NAME] [YOUR NUMBER]

### SMS / WhatsApp (after a call where they said yes)

> Hi [OWNER NAME], [YOUR NAME] here, thanks for the chat earlier. As promised, I'll [send your homepage mockup by DAY / see you at DAY TIME at BUSINESS / call you DAY at TIME]. Any questions before then, just give me a shout. Cheers!

### SMS / WhatsApp (sending a mockup)

> Hi [OWNER NAME], here's that rough homepage I put together for [BUSINESS]: [LINK]. It's only a draft, so I've used your [Facebook photos / Google reviews] as placeholders. Have a look on your phone when you get a minute. I'll give you a quick ring [DAY] to hear what you think.

> Hi [OWNER NAME],

>
> I noticed [BUSINESS] doesn't have its own website at the moment. You've got [your Facebook page / a Google listing / some great reviews], which is a brilliant start, but people searching for a [TRADE/TYPE OF BUSINESS] near Falmouth on their phone often go with whoever has a proper site they can check.
>

>

>
> Would a quick 15-minute chat this week suit, by phone or over a coffee?

> Cheers,
> [YOUR NAME]
> [YOUR BUSINESS NAME] · [YOUR NUMBER] · [YOUR WEBSITE]
>
> *P.S. A couple of local sites I've done: [LINK 1], [LINK 2].*

**Follow-up cadence (suggested):**
- **Day 0:** call, then voicemail + SMS if no answer
- **Day 2:** email
- **Day 5:** second call
- **Day 10:** final friendly SMS: *"Hi [NAME], last one from me! If a website's ever on your list, I'm local and happy to help. [YOUR NAME] [NUMBER]"*
- Then stop. Put them on a list to try again before the next season, with a reason (e.g. "ahead of Easter bookings").

## Timing guidance: when to call whom

These are rules of thumb based on how each type of business runs its day. Test them and keep notes on what works for you.

| Niche | Best times | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Trades** (plumbers, electricians, builders, roofers) | 7:30–8:15am before they start on site; 4:30–6pm as they finish; rainy days | Mid-morning or mid-afternoon (on a roof, or under a sink) |
| **Cafés** | 2:30–4pm after the lunch rush; first hour after opening on quiet days | 8–10am breakfast, 12–2pm lunch, weekends |
| **Restaurants & takeaways** | 2:30–4:30pm, between lunch and evening prep; Monday/Tuesday | 5:30pm onwards; Friday/Saturday |
| **Hair, beauty, barbers** | Just before opening; ask reception when the owner has a gap; their quiet day (often Monday or Tuesday) | Saturdays; late-night opening evenings |
| **B&Bs, guest houses, holiday lets** | 10:30am–12pm after breakfast and checkout; autumn/winter is ideal | 7–10am breakfast service; changeover days (often Saturday) |
| **Retail / independent shops** | 10–11:30am weekdays; Tuesday–Thursday | Lunch hour; Saturdays; summer holiday peak |
| **Marine / boat services** | Early morning; poor-weather days; off-season (October–March) | Fine summer days, regatta periods, Falmouth Week |
| **Cleaners, gardeners, dog groomers** | 8–9am or 5–7pm; dog groomers between appointments (ask) | Middle of the working day |
| **Garages & mechanics** | 8–8:45am; 4–5pm | Lunchtime; Saturday mornings (often busiest) |

**Seasonal angle for Falmouth and Penryn:**
- **October to March** is the best window for tourism businesses (cafés, B&Bs, marine, retail). They have time to talk and are already thinking about next season.
- **Late spring** is when the "we're about to get busy" urgency peaks. Use it, but expect them to have less time.
- **Summer** is when to call trades and locals-facing businesses. Leave hospitality alone unless they're clearly struggling.

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- Say you're local, and mean it. Mention real places (the Moor, Events Square, Penryn Town Hall, Ponsharden).
- Admit it's a cold call. Honesty disarms people.
- Ask for permission and give them a genuine way out.
- Look them up before you dial: their Facebook, Google reviews, what they do. Mention something specific.
- Use their words back to them in the pitch.
- Aim for a small next step (chat, visit, mockup), not a sale.
- Follow up exactly when you said you would.
- Keep notes on every call: name, best time, what they said.
- Check whether a number is registered with the TPS/CTPS before calling. In the UK, businesses can register with the Corporate TPS, and you shouldn't make unsolicited sales calls to registered numbers.[^2]

**Don't**
- Use fake urgency ("only two slots left!") or anything you can't back up.
- Slag off their Facebook page or their nephew's attempt at a website.
- Talk tech: no "SEO", "responsive", "CMS" unless they ask. Say "shows up on Google", "works on phones", "you can update it yourself".
- Ramble. If you've talked for over a minute without asking a question, stop.
- Push after a clear second "no". Thank them and leave the door open. Cornwall is small and word gets round, both ways.
- Call at their busiest times (see the table).
- Sound like you're reading. Learn the structure, not the words.
- Quote made-up stats. If you use a number, know where it came from.
