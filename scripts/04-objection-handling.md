# 04 – Objection Handling Bible
### Cold calling small local businesses in Penryn & Falmouth (no website yet)

> **Who this is for:** anyone ringing cafés, salons, trades, B&Bs, boat hire, gift shops, barbers, dog groomers, cleaners, tattooists, mechanics and the like around Penryn, Falmouth, Mylor, Flushing, Constantine, Mawnan Smith and Budock, who currently get by on Facebook, word of mouth, or a Google/Yell listing.
>
> **Tone:** friendly neighbour, not double-glazing salesman. You're a local helping a local. Slow down, smile down the phone, use their name, and never argue. If you "win" the argument you've lost the call.
>
> **Placeholders:** `[Name]` = you, `[Biz]` = their business, `[£price]` / `[£monthly]` = your real prices, `[example]` = a real local site you've built (use real ones only, never invent clients or results).

## Part 1 – The framework

### 1.1 The goal of the call is NOT to sell a website
On a cold call to a sole trader with flour on their hands, nobody buys a website. The only goal is **the next small yes**. Pick one before you dial:

| Small yes | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Free mock-up** (best) | "Can I knock up a quick one-page mock-up of what [Biz] could look like and WhatsApp you the link? No charge, no obligation." |
| **Pop-in** | "I'm in Penryn Thursday anyway. Could I pop in for 10 minutes and show you on my phone?" |
| **Proper call-back** | "What's a quieter time? I'll ring then, 10 minutes max, promise." |
| **Permission to text** | "Can I text you a couple of examples so you can have a look when you're not busy?" |

Every response in this document ends with a question that moves toward one of these.

### 1.2 The core loop: L-A-C-R-C (a UK-friendly LAARC)
Adapted from the LAARC model (Listen, Acknowledge, Assess, Respond, Confirm) and its cousin LAER (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond).

1. **Listen** – let them finish. Count "one-Falmouth" in your head before you speak. Silence feels long on the phone; it isn't.
2. **Acknowledge** – take the heat out. Don't agree with the conclusion, agree that the concern is fair.
   *"Fair enough." / "That's completely reasonable." / "Yeah, I hear that a lot, to be honest."*
3. **Clarify (Assess/Explore)** – find out what's really going on with a soft question.
   *"Can I ask, is that more about the cost, or more that you just don't see the point?"*
4. **Respond** – answer the *real* concern in one or two sentences. No speeches.
5. **Confirm & move** – check it landed, then ask for the small yes.
   *"Does that make sense? So would a free mock-up be worth a look?"*

### 1.3 Isolate-and-confirm
Most first objections are reflexes, not reasons. Before you answer anything big, isolate it:

> "Totally fair. Just so I don't waste your time, if the price was right and it was no hassle for you, is there anything else that'd stop you having a look?"

- If they say **"No, it's just the money"** → you now know the only real objection. Handle it, then close on the small yes.
- If they say **"Well, also…"** → good, the real objection is coming out. Go back to Clarify.

### 1.4 Feel-Felt-Found (said like a human)
Buyers recognise the literal words "I understand how you feel." Use the structure, not the script:

- **Feel:** "That makes total sense…"
- **Felt:** "…a lot of the people I've spoken to round here said exactly the same, [example type, e.g. a café on the high street]…"
- **Found:** "…what they found was [real, specific outcome you can stand behind]."

Only use "Found" with a *true* story. If you don't have one yet, use "what I'd expect" or "what the numbers tend to show" instead. Never invent clients.

### 1.5 NEPQ-style questions (let them sell themselves)
Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning, popularised by Jeremy Miner, is about asking calm, curious questions so the prospect states the problem in their own words. You aren't pushing; you're helping them think. Useful ones for this market:

- **Situation:** "How do most new customers find you at the moment?"
- **Problem-awareness:** "What happens when someone who's never heard of you searches for a [dog groomer] in Falmouth?"
- **Consequence:** "If the summer's quiet next year, what would you do to fill the diary?"
- **Solution-awareness:** "If people could see your prices and book without ringing you, would that save you any time?"
- **Commitment (soft):** "Would it be worth seeing what that might look like, no strings?"

Tone matters more than the words: slightly slower, slightly lower, genuinely curious. Use "Can I ask…" and "Just out of interest…" a lot.

### 1.6 Ten tone rules for Cornwall
1. **Ask permission early.** "Have you got 30 seconds, or have I caught you mid-something?" Earns more time than it costs.
2. **Be local and specific.** Mention streets, landmarks, seasons: "Market Street", "the Moor", "Commercial Road", "Falmouth Week", "the summer crowds". Only if true for you.
3. **No jargon.** Say "show up on Google" not "SEO". "Works on phones" not "responsive". "Your own web address" not "domain".
4. **Lower the stakes constantly.** "No pressure." "Have a look, if it's not for you, bin it."
5. **Respect the craft.** They're the expert in their trade. You're the expert in getting found.
6. **Never knock their Facebook/Google listing.** Build on it: "That's a brilliant start, a website just ties it all together."
7. **Never knock competitors or the nephew.** Ever. Cornwall is small and everyone's somebody's cousin.
8. **Laugh with them.** If they joke, joke back. If they're grumpy, be warm, not chirpy.
9. **Two tries, then go.** Handle an objection twice at most. A third push turns "not now" into "never".
10. **Leave every call so they'd take your next one.** That's how you win in a town this size.

### 1.7 Compliance basics (UK)
- **Screen numbers against the TPS and CTPS** before calling. Under PECR you must not make unsolicited marketing calls to numbers registered there (sole traders and partnerships can register on TPS; companies on CTPS), unless they've told you that you can.
- **Always give your name and business name**, and a contact number if asked.
- **If someone says "don't call me again", log it and never call again.** See Part 3.
- Don't overclaim. No "guaranteed first page of Google", no made-up stats or reviews.

### A. Brush-offs & timing

#### 1. "Not interested."
**What's really behind it:** A reflex. They don't know what you're offering yet; they're protecting their time from "another sales call."
**Say:** "No worries at all, I'd probably say the same if a stranger rang me. Can I just ask, is that not interested in a website at all, or just not interested in a sales call right now?"
**Or:** "Fair enough. I'll be really quick so you can get on: I build simple websites for local businesses in Penryn and Falmouth that don't have one, and I noticed [Biz] is just on Facebook. Is that something you've ever thought about, or genuinely not on the radar?"
**Move it forward:** "If I made a quick mock-up for free and texted it over, would you at least have a nose at it?"

#### 2. "Just send me an email."
**What's really behind it:** Polite exit 80% of the time. Sometimes genuine: they prefer to read things in their own time.
**Say:** "Course, happy to. So I don't send you a generic one you'll delete, can I ask one quick question: how do most new customers find you at the moment?"
**Or:** "Yep, no problem. Rather than an email full of waffle, would it be more useful if I sent a link to a quick mock-up of what *your* site could look like? Then you've actually got something to look at."
**Move it forward:** "What's the best email? And if I give you a quick ring Thursday to see what you thought, is that alright?"

#### 3. "I'm busy." / "Bad time."
**What's really behind it:** Often literally true (small business owners are doing five jobs). Sometimes a brush-off.
**Say:** "Sorry, I've caught you at a bad moment, I'll be two seconds. When's normally a quieter time, first thing or after you close?"
**Or:** "No problem at all. Thirty-second version: free mock-up website for [Biz], no obligation. Can I text it to you to look at later?"
**Move it forward:** "Is this the best number to text, or is there a better one?"

#### 4. "Call back later."
**What's really behind it:** Could be real or could be a polite fob-off. "Later" needs a time or it means "never".
**Say:** "Will do. Is there a time that actually suits, so I'm not ringing you in the middle of a rush? Tuesday afternoon, say?"
**Or:** "Happy to. Just so I'm not wasting your time when I call back, is a website something you'd consider, or would you rather I didn't bother?"
**Move it forward:** "Lovely, so [day] at [time]. I'll pop a text the morning before so it doesn't come out of nowhere. Alright?"

#### 5. "Just send me some info / a leaflet."
**What's really behind it:** Wants something tangible without committing. Or wants you gone.
**Say:** "Sure. Honestly though, a leaflet about websites in general won't tell you much. What would actually help is seeing *your* business on a page. Shall I do that instead? Free, takes me a day or two."
**Move it forward:** "If you like what you see, we chat. If not, no harm done. Fair?"

#### 6. "How did you get my number?"
**What's really behind it:** Suspicion, privacy worry. Needs a straight, transparent answer.
**Say:** "Good question. It's the number on your Facebook page / Google listing. I'm going through local businesses in Falmouth that don't have a website yet. If you'd rather I didn't ring this number, just say and I'll take it off my list, no problem."
**Move it forward:** "Is this the best way to reach you, or is there a better time or number?"

#### 7. "Is this a sales call?"
**What's really behind it:** They want honesty. Dodging this question kills trust instantly.
**Say:** "Ha, yes, it is, I won't pretend otherwise! But it's a short one. I'm [Name], I'm local, I build websites for small businesses round here. Can I have 30 seconds and you can tell me to sling my hook?"
**Move it forward:** "How do most new customers find you at the moment?"

#### 8. "Take me off your list." / "Don't call me again."
**What's really behind it:** A clear no. Respect it fully. (This is also a legal point; see 1.7.)
**Say:** "Absolutely, I'll do that now. Sorry to have bothered you, and all the best with [Biz]."
**Move it forward:** Don't. Log it and end the call warmly. (This one is in the walk-away list.)

#### 9. "Speak to me after Christmas / in the new year."
**What's really behind it:** Seasonal overload, or a soft stall. Note: January can be a good time to start, since the site can be ready for Easter.
**Say:** "Makes total sense, it's mad this time of year. Funnily enough, January's when a lot of people get this sorted so it's live before Easter and the first visitors arrive. Shall I pencil in the second week of January?"
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if I sent a mock-up now, so you've got something to mull over during the quiet bit?"

#### 10. "I'll think about it."
**What's really behind it:** There's an unspoken objection: price, trust, partner, or "I just don't want to say no to your face."
**Say:** "Course, it's not something to rush. Can I ask, what's the bit you'd want to think over? Is it the cost, whether it'd actually bring anything in, or something else?"
**Or:** "No worries. Usually when people say that, there's one thing nagging at them. What's yours?" (said with a smile)
**Move it forward:** "If I sent you a mock-up and a price on one page, would that make the thinking easier? Then I'll ring Friday to see where you're at."

#### 11. "I'll look you up / I'll be in touch."
**What's really behind it:** Polite goodbye. They won't remember your name by teatime.
**Say:** "Brilliant, thank you. Just in case it slips your mind (it'd slip mine!) can I text you my details and a couple of examples so they're on your phone?"
**Move it forward:** "And if I haven't heard by next week, is it alright if I give you a quick nudge?"

### B. Money

#### 12. "It's too expensive."
**What's really behind it:** Either (a) they don't see the value yet, (b) they're comparing to "free" Facebook, or (c) a genuine cash-flow issue. Find out which.
**Say:** "Fair, and I'd rather you said that than not. Can I ask, too expensive compared to what? Is it the upfront bit or the monthly bit?"
**Or:** "I get it. Think of it this way: if the site brought in [one extra booking / one boiler job / two haircuts] a month, would it be worth it then?"
**Move it forward:** "What sort of figure did you have in your head? Let's see if there's a version that fits."

#### 13. "I can't afford it right now."
**What's really behind it:** Real cash-flow worry (common after a quiet winter) or a polite no.
**Say:** "Totally understand, it's been a tough couple of years for loads of people round here. We can spread it: [£monthly] a month instead of a lump sum. Would that make a difference?"
**Or:** "That's fair. Can I ask, if it paid for itself within a few months, would it be affordable then? Because that's really the question."
**Move it forward:** "When does it usually pick up for you, Easter? Shall we look at it a month before so it's ready?"

#### 14. "My nephew / son / mate can do it cheaper."
**What's really behind it:** They trust family, and it feels free. But "the nephew" often never gets round to it, or builds it then goes off to uni.
**Say:** "Brilliant, honestly, if he's good, go with him! The only thing I'd ask is, has he started yet? Because a lot of people I speak to have had 'the nephew's doing it' for a couple of years."
**Or:** "That's great. Just worth asking him a few things: who'll keep it updated, who owns the web address, and what happens if he moves away? If he's got all that covered, you're sorted."
**Move it forward:** "Tell you what, I'll send a free mock-up anyway, so you've got something to compare. If his is better, no hard feelings. Deal?"

#### 15. "Wix / Squarespace / GoDaddy is free (or dirt cheap)."
**What's really behind it:** They've seen the adverts. They don't count their own time, or the cost of a site that never ranks or never gets finished.
**Say:** "Yeah, they're good tools, I'm not going to knock them. The thing is the software's cheap; the time isn't. Most people I talk to started one, got three pages in, and gave up. How many evenings have you got spare?"
**Or:** "True. And for some people that's perfect. The difference with me is I do the lot: words, photos, getting you on Google, and I'm local if anything goes wrong. You don't touch it unless you want to."
**Move it forward:** "Have you had a go yourself yet, or has it been on the to-do list?"

#### 16. "How much is it?" (asked in the first 20 seconds)
**What's really behind it:** Could be a buying signal or a way to dismiss you on price before seeing value. Answer honestly but give context.
**Say:** "Straight answer: most of my local sites are between [£low] and [£high], or [£monthly] a month. It depends on what you need. A simple one for a café is at the lower end. Can I ask what you'd want it to do, so I can give you a proper figure?"
**Move it forward:** "Would it help to see a mock-up before we talk numbers properly?"

#### 17. "I don't want monthly fees."
**What's really behind it:** Subscription fatigue; fear of being locked in.
**Say:** "Completely get that, everything's a subscription these days. You can pay once upfront if you'd prefer. The only ongoing bit is the web address and hosting, which is about [£x] a year, like paying for a phone line. No long contracts."
**Move it forward:** "Would a one-off price suit you better?"

#### 18. "What's the catch with this free mock-up?"
**What's really behind it:** "Free" triggers suspicion, rightly.
**Say:** "Ha, fair question! No catch. It takes me an hour or two, and it's the easiest way for you to see if you like my work. If you don't, you've lost nothing and I've had some practice. If you do, we chat."
**Move it forward:** "So, can I grab a couple of your Facebook photos to use on it?"

#### 19. "Times are tough." / "Business rates, energy bills, cost of living…"
**What's really behind it:** Genuine stress. Show empathy before anything else.
**Say:** "Honestly, I hear that from nearly everyone this year. That's actually why a lot of my customers did it: when the town's quieter, you need to be the one people find first. It doesn't have to be a big spend."
**Move it forward:** "What's been hitting you hardest? Is it fewer customers, or the same customers spending less?"

#### 20. "I've seen it for £50 on Fiverr / got a cheaper quote."
**What's really behind it:** Price-shopping. They may not be comparing like for like.
**Say:** "You'll definitely find it cheaper, no argument there. The question is what's included. Does the £50 one include the words, getting you on Google, someone local to ring when it breaks, and updates? If it does, take it!"
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if I sent you a one-page list of what's included, so you can compare it properly?"

#### 21. "How do I know it'll pay for itself?"
**What's really behind it:** A good, sensible business question. This is a buying signal.
**Say:** "Great question, and I won't promise you miracles. What I can say is that most people search online before they choose a local business now. A website makes sure you're there when they do. How much is one new customer worth to you, roughly?"
**Move it forward:** "So if it brought in [x] extra customers a year, it's paid for. Does that seem doable to you?"

### C. "We don't need one"

#### 22. "I'm busy enough already."
**What's really behind it:** Pride, and fear of being overwhelmed. Often true in summer, less so in February.
**Say:** "That's brilliant, genuinely, good on you. Can I ask, is it the same in January and February? Because that's usually when people wish they had something bringing in enquiries."
**Or:** "Love that. A website isn't only about more customers, it can be *better* customers or less time on the phone: prices, opening hours and FAQs all online so you're not answering the same questions."
**Move it forward:** "How much time a week do you spend answering 'are you open?' or 'how much is…' messages?"

#### 23. "Word of mouth works for us."
**What's really behind it:** It does work, and they're proud of it. Don't fight it; build on it.
**Say:** "Word of mouth is the best advertising there is, no question. Here's the thing though: when someone hears 'go to [Biz]', what's the first thing they do? They Google you. A website is where word of mouth lands."
**Or:** "Absolutely. And a website just makes word of mouth easier to pass on. Instead of 'ask for Dave, he's great, I think his number's somewhere', it's 'look up [Biz]'."
**Move it forward:** "When someone recommends you, what do they find if they search for you right now?"

#### 24. "Facebook is enough."
**What's really behind it:** It's free, familiar, and it's working a bit. They don't see the gaps.
**Say:** "Facebook's great for keeping regulars in the loop, I'd keep it going. The catch is that you don't own it. Your posts only reach a slice of your followers, and if your account gets hacked or locked, your shop window's gone overnight. A website's yours."
**Or:** "Totally. The only thing is, not everyone's on Facebook, and people searching Google for '[service] Falmouth' often don't see Facebook pages near the top. The website catches those people and points them back to your Facebook."
**Move it forward:** "Out of interest, have you ever had a customer say they couldn't find you, or didn't have Facebook?"

#### 25. "Our Google listing is enough."
**What's really behind it:** Their Google Business Profile does bring calls. They think that's the whole job.
**Say:** "Your Google listing is brilliant, honestly that's the most important bit and you've already got it. A website makes it work harder. Google likes listings that link to a proper website, and when people tap through they get the full picture: prices, photos, booking."
**Or:** "Yeah, and that's a great start. The trouble is a listing only shows a few lines. If your competitor in Falmouth has a listing *and* a website, who looks more established?"
**Move it forward:** "When people find you on Google, what do they usually ask when they ring? Because that's what we'd put on the website."

#### 26. "My customers are locals / older. They don't use the internet."
**What's really behind it:** Partly true, but usually underestimated. Their kids and grandkids book for them, and newcomers to the area search online.
**Say:** "That's fair, and I'd never want you to lose the personal touch. Funnily enough, a lot of older customers get their son or daughter to look things up for them. And everyone who's just moved to Falmouth, or the students and their parents, will be searching online."
**Or:** "Makes sense. Do you get many new customers, though, or is it all the same faces? Because the website's really for the people who don't know you yet."
**Move it forward:** "Where do your *new* customers come from at the moment?"

#### 27. "I've done fine for 20 years without one."
**What's really behind it:** Pride and proof of a good business. They don't want to be told they're doing it wrong.
**Say:** "And that says everything about how good you are. Twenty years on reputation is brilliant. I'm not saying you're doing it wrong at all. It's just that the next 20 might look a bit different, with more people finding businesses on their phones."
**Move it forward:** "Are you seeing as many new faces as you did ten years ago?"

#### 28. "I'm in Yell / the directories."
**What's really behind it:** They pay (or paid) for a listing and feel covered.
**Say:** "That's good, every bit helps. The thing with directories is you're listed right next to all your competitors. A website is just *you*, no distractions. And it costs about the same over a year as some directory packages."
**Move it forward:** "Do you know roughly how many enquiries you get from Yell?"

#### 29. "We're on Instagram, that's our website."
**What's really behind it:** Common with cafés, salons, tattooists, florists, makers. Visual businesses love Instagram, rightly.
**Say:** "Your Instagram looks great. Keep it, it's your gallery. The website's your shop counter: prices, opening hours, booking, how to find you. And it shows up on Google, which Instagram mostly doesn't."
**Move it forward:** "Do people ever DM you asking for prices or opening times? That's the bit a website handles for you."

#### 30. "We're too small for a website."
**What's really behind it:** They think websites are for "proper companies". It's a confidence thing.
**Say:** "Honestly, small businesses are exactly who I build for, mostly one-person and two-person businesses round Penryn and Falmouth. It doesn't need to be fancy, just one or two pages saying who you are, what you do, and how to get hold of you."
**Move it forward:** "If it was just a simple one-pager, would that feel more like your size?"

#### 31. "People just ring me."
**What's really behind it:** The phone works; they don't see a problem.
**Say:** "That's great, and a website makes sure even more people ring you. Plus, the people who don't like ringing (and a lot of younger people don't) can message or book instead."
**Move it forward:** "Do you ever miss calls when you're busy? What happens to those people?"

#### 32. "Nobody uses websites any more, it's all TikTok and apps."
**What's really behind it:** Media headlines. Half-true for some audiences.
**Say:** "Ha, I can see why you'd think that! But when people actually decide to *buy* from a local business, most still Google it and look at a website and reviews. Social media gets attention; the website gets the booking."
**Move it forward:** "When you last looked for a local business you hadn't used before, what did you do?"

#### 33. "I don't want more customers, I can't handle more."
**What's really behind it:** They're at capacity. More customers = more stress.
**Say:** "That's a nice problem to have! A website can help with that too. You can raise your prices a bit and fill your diary with better jobs, put up waiting lists, or cut the time-wasting calls. It's not just about volume."
**Move it forward:** "If you could pick and choose your work a bit more, what would you do less of?"

### D. Trust & bad past experiences

#### 34. "I got burned by a web company before."
**What's really behind it:** Real pain: paid for a site that never ranked, got locked into a contract, couldn't get hold of anyone, or lost the domain. This is gold, because they already believe in websites.
**Say:** "Oh no, I'm really sorry, I hear that a lot and it makes me cross, honestly. Can I ask what went wrong? I'd just like to know so I don't make the same mistakes."
*(Listen fully. Then:)* "Right, so the way I work is: you own your web address and your site outright, no long contracts, and I'm local, so you can actually knock on my door. Does that sound different?"
**Move it forward:** "If I could show you a site I've done for someone round here and you could ring them, would that help?"

#### 35. "Is this a scam?"
**What's really behind it:** Fair suspicion; scam calls are everywhere. Don't get offended.
**Say:** "Ha, good on you for asking, there's loads of dodgy calls about. I'm [Name], I'm based in [Penryn/Falmouth], you can look me up at [your site] and see sites I've done. I'm not asking for any money or details today, just whether you'd like to see a free mock-up."
**Move it forward:** "Would you be more comfortable if I texted my details first so you can check me out?"

#### 36. "Who are you? Never heard of you."
**What's really behind it:** Trust gap. They need to place you.
**Say:** "Fair point, I'm not exactly famous! I'm [Name], I live in [area], and I build websites for small local businesses. You might have seen [example, only if real]. I'm small, which means you deal with me, not a call centre."
**Move it forward:** "Can I send you a couple of examples so you can see what I do?"

#### 37. "How do I know you'll still be around in two years?"
**What's really behind it:** Fear of being left with an orphaned website.
**Say:** "Really sensible question. Two things: first, you own the site and the web address, so if I got hit by a bus tomorrow (touch wood) any web person could pick it up. Second, I'm local and not going anywhere. I'll put that in writing."
**Move it forward:** "Would that peace of mind make you more comfortable taking a look?"

#### 38. "I had a website before, it didn't bring in anything."
**What's really behind it:** The old site was probably not on Google properly, out of date, or not phone-friendly. They think "websites don't work".
**Say:** "That's frustrating, and I believe you. Can I ask, did it show up when you searched for your business on Google? And did it work properly on phones? Because that's usually the reason they don't bring anything in."
**Move it forward:** "If I could show you how yours would show up locally, would that be worth five minutes?"

#### 39. "Have you got any examples / references?"
**What's really behind it:** A buying signal! They're interested enough to check.
**Say:** "Absolutely. I'll text you a couple now. [Example] is a good one, and I'm sure they'd be happy to have a chat if you want." *(Only reference clients who've agreed.)*
**Move it forward:** "And while you look at those, shall I put together a quick mock-up for [Biz] too?"

#### 40. "I don't buy anything over the phone."
**What's really behind it:** A personal rule, often after getting stung. Respect it.
**Say:** "Very wise, and I'm not asking you to buy anything today. All I'd like to do is send a free mock-up, or pop in and show you face to face. You'd decide in your own time."
**Move it forward:** "Would popping in suit you better? I'm round your way [day]."

#### 41. "I don't want to get tied into a contract / hidden fees."
**What's really behind it:** Fear of the classic "36-month lease" web deals.
**Say:** "Completely agree, I hate that too. No long contracts with me. You'll get one price on one page with everything listed. If anything's extra, I'll tell you before, not after."
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if I sent that one-page price breakdown with the mock-up?"

### E. Authority / decision-maker

#### 42. "I need to ask my partner / wife / husband."
**What's really behind it:** Genuine joint decision, or a polite out. Either way, help them have that conversation.
**Say:** "Course, it's a joint decision. What do you think they'd say? Is there anything you think they'd be worried about?"
**Or:** "Totally fair. Would it be easier if I sent the mock-up so you can both look at it together over a cuppa? Or I could pop in when you're both around."
**Move it forward:** "When are you likely to see them? Shall I ring Thursday once you've had a chat?"

#### 43. "The owner's not here." / "I just work here."
**What's really behind it:** You're talking to staff. Make them an ally, not a gatekeeper to beat.
**Say:** "No worries at all, thanks for picking up. What's their name, and when's the best time to catch them? And just out of interest, do customers ever ask you if you've got a website?"
**Move it forward:** "Could I leave my number with you, or is there a better way to reach them?"

#### 44. "My accountant / bookkeeper deals with that kind of thing."
**What's really behind it:** Deflecting to someone "official". Accountants rarely choose websites.
**Say:** "Makes sense to run it past them on cost. The website itself, though, is really about your business and your customers, and you're the expert there. Shall I send the mock-up and price so you've got something to show them?"
**Move it forward:** "If the numbers stacked up for them, would it be a yes from you?"

#### 45. "Head office / the franchise handles that."
**What's really behind it:** True for franchises. Possibly a polite no.
**Say:** "Ah, fair enough. Do they give you your own local page, or is it one main site? Some franchises let you have a local page for Falmouth, and that's where I'd help."
**Move it forward:** "If it's not allowed, no problem at all. Is there anyone local you'd recommend I talk to?"

### F. Competitor & DIY

#### 46. "I'm already talking to someone."
**What's really behind it:** Might be true (good, they're a real buyer) or a fob-off.
**Say:** "Oh great, so you've decided you want one, that's half the battle. How's that going? Have they shown you anything yet?"
**Or:** "Fair play. It's always worth having a second option to compare though. Shall I send a free mock-up so you can see two side by side? No pressure either way."
**Move it forward:** "When are you hoping to decide?"

#### 47. "I'm doing it myself."
**What's really behind it:** Pride, budget, or control. Often stuck halfway.
**Say:** "Good on you! How's it going? The bit most people get stuck on is getting it to show on Google, so if you ever want a hand with just that, give me a shout."
**Or:** "Brilliant. Can I ask, how long have you been working on it? Because if it's been on the to-do list a while, I can take it off your plate."
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if I had a look at what you've got so far and gave you a couple of free tips?"

#### 48. "I've got one half-built / started ages ago."
**What's really behind it:** Embarrassment. It's stalled and they know it.
**Say:** "That's really common, honestly. Life gets in the way. Would you like me to finish it off for you, or start fresh? Whatever's easier."
**Move it forward:** "Can you send me the link so I can see where it's at?"

#### 49. "A friend's a designer, they'll do it eventually."
**What's really behind it:** Loyalty plus a stalled project. Same as the nephew.
**Say:** "Lovely, always good to support friends. If they're snowed under, though, I'm happy to step in, or even work with them. How long has 'eventually' been so far?" (said warmly)
**Move it forward:** "Shall I send you a mock-up so you've got a back-up if their timing doesn't work out?"

#### 50. "I'll just use ChatGPT / AI to make one."
**What's really behind it:** They've heard AI can build sites now. Partly true.
**Say:** "You can definitely get a basic page out of AI now, it's impressive. The bit it won't do is know your business, get your photos looking good, set up your web address and Google properly, and be there when something breaks. That's what I do."
**Move it forward:** "Have you given it a go yet? How did it come out?"

### G. Technical fears & hassle

#### 51. "I can't update it, I'm not techy."
**What's really behind it:** Fear of looking stupid, or of having a site that goes stale.
**Say:** "You don't need to be. I do the updates. You just text me, 'new menu' or 'closed Monday' and I sort it. Or, if you can post on Facebook, you can update this, I'll show you."
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if updates were just a text away?"

#### 52. "I don't want the hassle."
**What's really behind it:** They imagine weeks of meetings, writing and technical stuff.
**Say:** "That's exactly what I take off you. Honestly, it's one chat, maybe half an hour, about your business, then I do the lot. Words, photos, set-up. You just say 'yep' or 'change that bit'."
**Move it forward:** "If it was that easy, would it be worth doing?"

#### 53. "What about hosting and domains? I don't understand all that."
**What's really behind it:** Jargon fear and fear of hidden costs.
**Say:** "Good question. The domain's just your web address, like [biz]falmouth.co.uk. Hosting's like the rent for it to sit on the internet. I sort both, it's in the price, and the web address is registered in *your* name so you own it."
**Move it forward:** "Have you got a web address already, or shall I check if a good one's free?"

#### 54. "I haven't got any photos or anything to write."
**What's really behind it:** Overwhelm at the "homework".
**Say:** "Don't worry, most people haven't. I can use the photos from your Facebook, or pop round and take some on a nice day. And I'll write the words from a quick chat with you."
**Move it forward:** "Can I use some of your Facebook photos for the mock-up?"

#### 55. "What about GDPR, cookies, all that legal stuff?"
**What's really behind it:** Worry about doing something wrong.
**Say:** "Sensible to ask. I set up the basics properly, a privacy page and a cookie notice if you need one, so you're covered. Keep it simple, and there's not much to worry about." *(Don't give legal advice beyond what you know.)*
**Move it forward:** "Would you want customers to be able to contact you through it, or just ring?"

#### 56. "What if it breaks or gets hacked?"
**What's really behind it:** Fear of being left alone with a problem.
**Say:** "Then you ring me and I fix it. I keep it updated and backed up, so it's very unlikely, and I'm local, so you're not stuck in a queue."
**Move it forward:** "Would having a local person to ring make you feel better about it?"

#### 57. "I haven't got time to be involved."
**What's really behind it:** Truly time-poor. Needs reassurance on effort.
**Say:** "Totally get it. I'll need about 20 minutes of you, total, and I can do it over the phone in the evening or while you're on a break. Everything else is me."
**Move it forward:** "When's your quietest 20 minutes in the week?"

### H. Seasonal & Cornwall-specific

#### 58. "We're quiet in winter, no point."
**What's really behind it:** Cash is tight now, and they don't think online helps in the off-season.
**Say:** "That's actually why winter's a good time to do it. You've got a bit more headspace, and it's ready and climbing up Google before the Easter crowds. People start planning their Cornwall trips months ahead."
**Or:** "Fair. And a website can help the quiet months: gift vouchers, winter offers, things that bring locals in when the visitors have gone."
**Move it forward:** "Would it be worth getting it ready for, say, February half-term?"

#### 59. "The tourists find us anyway."
**What's really behind it:** In summer, footfall is easy. They don't see the visitors who went elsewhere.
**Say:** "In August, I'm sure they do, Falmouth's rammed! But a lot of visitors decide where to eat or what to book *before* they arrive, on their phone in the car or the night before. If you're not there, they've already picked someone else."
**Move it forward:** "Do you ever get people say 'we were looking for you but couldn't find you online'?"

#### 60. "I'm retiring soon / selling up."
**What's really behind it:** Why spend money on a business they're leaving?
**Say:** "Oh, congratulations! Funnily enough, that's when a website can really help. Businesses with a proper online presence and reviews are easier to sell and can look more established to buyers. It's part of the shop window."
**Or:** "Makes sense. Is it a couple of years off, or more like months? If it's months, then fair enough, I'll leave you be."
**Move it forward:** "Would a simple site that helps you get a better price when you sell be worth a look?"

#### 61. "We're flat out in summer, ring me in the winter."
**What's really behind it:** Genuine. Mid-season is chaos.
**Say:** "Completely understand, I won't keep you. Shall I ring you in, say, October once it calms down? I'll stick it in the diary."
**Move it forward:** "Is it alright if I send a mock-up in the meantime so you can look at it when you've got a minute?"

#### 62. "We're booked through Airbnb / Booking.com, we don't need our own site."
**What's really behind it:** The platforms work. They're paying commission but it's invisible.
**Say:** "Makes sense, they bring the bookings. The only thing is the commission they take. If even your returning guests booked direct through your own site, how much would that save you a year?"
**Move it forward:** "Roughly what percentage do they take from you? Let's see if a site would pay for itself."

#### 63. "Falmouth / Penryn's small, everyone knows us."
**What's really behind it:** True for long-term locals. Forgets newcomers, students, second-home owners, visitors, and people from Truro/Helston.
**Say:** "Everyone *local* does, definitely, and that's brilliant. But every September a few thousand new students and their parents arrive, plus people moving down, plus the visitors. They don't know you yet. That's who the website's for."
**Move it forward:** "Do you get many customers from Truro, Helston or further out?"

#### 64. "We're on the high street, people just walk past and come in."
**What's really behind it:** Footfall-dependent shops. They don't see digital as relevant.
**Say:** "Footfall's gold, and you've got a great spot. A website just catches the people who aren't walking past right now: the ones planning a day out, or looking for somewhere when it's chucking it down."
**Move it forward:** "What happens on the rainy days in winter when the street's empty?"

#### 65. "It's all students round here, they don't spend."
**What's really behind it:** Frustration with the student market in Penryn. But students search online for everything, and their parents visit with money.
**Say:** "Ha, I hear you! But students live on their phones, so if you want any of them, that's where they look. And when the parents come down for move-in or graduation, they're searching for somewhere nice to eat or stay."
**Move it forward:** "Would graduation week or freshers' be worth targeting?"

### I. Tradespeople-specific

#### 66. "I'm on a roof right now." / "I'm under a sink."
**What's really behind it:** Literally true. Safety first. Make it easy to say yes to a text.
**Say:** "Oh, get off the phone, I don't want you falling! Can I text you instead? Two lines, look when you're down."
**Move it forward:** "What time do you usually finish, so I can ring when you're in the van?"

#### 67. "I get all my work through Checkatrade / MyBuilder / Rated People / Bark."
**What's really behind it:** The platforms work, but they're paying monthly and/or per lead, and competing with everyone else on the same page.
**Say:** "Good, those are useful. Can I ask, how much a month are you paying all in, with the leads? Because a website's a one-off that's yours, and it sends people to you without paying per job or competing on the same page as five other plumbers."
**Or:** "Keep your Checkatrade, the reviews are great. Link it on your own site, so people see the reviews *and* call you direct."
**Move it forward:** "If you could get even a few jobs a month direct without paying for the lead, would that be worth it?"

#### 68. "I'm booked up for months."
**What's really behind it:** Pride and capacity. Not a problem they feel right now.
**Say:** "That's brilliant, means you're good at what you do. A website lets you be choosy: bigger jobs, better areas, and put your prices up. It also stops the phone ringing with stuff you don't do."
**Move it forward:** "What kind of jobs would you love more of?"

#### 69. "Internet leads are all tyre-kickers and price shoppers."
**What's really behind it:** They've had bad leads from the platforms. Fair point.
**Say:** "Platform leads definitely can be. With your own site, people have already seen your work, your reviews and your rough prices before they ring, so they've half chosen you. Less haggling."
**Move it forward:** "Would putting a 'jobs from £x' on the site help filter people out?"

#### 70. "My van's my advert."
**What's really behind it:** It works locally and they paid good money for the wrap.
**Say:** "Love it, and the van's a moving billboard. What do people do when they see the van and want to remember you later? They search your name. If there's a website with your work on, you've got them."
**Move it forward:** "Shall I put the web address on a design for your van, so it all ties together?"

#### 71. "I only work for builders / agents / subbies, I don't need the public."
**What's really behind it:** B2B work. A public site seems irrelevant.
**Say:** "Fair, that's a different game. Even then, contractors and letting agents Google you before they use you. A simple site with your work, insurance and certifications makes you look the proper firm."
**Move it forward:** "Do you ever get asked for photos of past jobs or your certificates?"

#### 72. "I don't do computers. Ring the missus / my partner does the books."
**What's really behind it:** Delegation, sometimes a fob-off. Partner is often the real decision-maker for admin.
**Say:** "No problem at all. What's their name, and what's the best number? I'll keep it short."
**Move it forward:** "Is it alright if I mention you said it's worth a look?"

### J. Curveballs

#### 73. "Can you guarantee I'll be top of Google?"
**What's really behind it:** They've been promised it before. A test of honesty.
**Say:** "Honestly? No, and anyone who guarantees that is fibbing. What I can do is set it up properly so you've got a really good chance of showing for '[service] Falmouth', and tie it in with your Google listing. Better to under-promise."
**Move it forward:** "What would someone type in to find a business like yours?"

#### 74. "Why should I use you rather than a big company?"
**What's really behind it:** A buying signal; they're comparing.
**Say:** "Good question. With a big company you're a number. With me, you've got one person who knows Falmouth, picks up the phone, and you can meet for a coffee. And usually it's cheaper, because there's no fancy office."
**Move it forward:** "What matters most to you, price, speed, or having someone to ring?"

#### 75. "Just make one and if I like it I'll pay."
**What's really behind it:** A buying signal wrapped in caution.
**Say:** "That's basically what the free mock-up is! I'll do the homepage for free. If you like it, we agree a price and I finish it. If not, no harm done."
**Move it forward:** "Brilliant. What three things do you want people to know about [Biz]?"

#### 76. "I'll do it next year."
**What's really behind it:** Procrastination; no urgency.
**Say:** "Fair enough. Can I ask, what'll be different next year? Because if it's not money or time, it'd be nice to have it working for you this year instead."
**Move it forward:** "Would it help if we started small, just a one-page site now, and built on it later?"

## Part 3 – When to walk away gracefully

Walking away well is a skill. In a town the size of Falmouth, your reputation travels faster than your website portfolio.

### Walk away immediately when:
1. **They say "don't call me again" or "take me off your list".** Apologise, confirm, log it, never call again. (Legal and decent.)
2. **The number is on TPS/CTPS** and you've called by mistake. Apologise and end the call.
3. **They're clearly distressed**: bereavement, illness, a crisis in the shop. "Sorry, I've caught you at a really bad time. Take care of yourself." End there.
4. **They're unsafe to talk**: driving without hands-free, up a ladder, operating machinery.
5. **They're rude or aggressive.** Stay polite, end quickly, don't bite.
6. **They're closing the business soon** (months, not years), or it's already sold.

### Walk away for now (with the door open) when:
7. **You've handled the same objection twice** and they're still saying no. Third push = annoyance.
8. **They give a firm, reasoned no** ("We've genuinely thought about it and it's not for us").
9. **They can't name a single new customer they want**, i.e. they truly don't want growth.
10. **The budget is genuinely zero** and they're struggling to pay bills. Selling to them would be wrong.

### The graceful exit script
> "No problem at all, I really appreciate you giving me a couple of minutes. Can I just leave my number with you, so if anything changes, or the nephew doesn't come through (ha!), you know where I am? Have a lovely day, and good luck with the summer."

### Things to do after a walk-away
- **Log the reason** (not interested / timing / retired / do-not-call). Respect do-not-call permanently.
- **For "not now"**, set a reminder 3–6 months out (aim for October–February for seasonal businesses).
- **Never** send more than one follow-up text after a polite no.
- **Never** post about or mock a prospect anywhere, ever.
