# Offer & Pricing Talk Tracks

How to present the offer on the phone, in a visit, or in a follow-up. Use it alongside `00-master-cold-call-script.md` (framework) and `01-niche-scripts.md` (niche pitches).

**Golden rule:** don't lead with price. Lead with *their* problem, show them the fix (ideally a mockup), and only then talk money. When you do talk money, be clear, confident and straightforward. People round here respect someone who just tells them the price.

All prices are placeholders: `[PRICE]`, `[STARTER PRICE]`, `[MONTHLY PRICE]`, etc. Set your own, write them in, and practise saying them out loud until they don't feel awkward.

---

## 1. The free homepage mockup ("I already made you a draft")

This is your strongest tool. It moves the conversation from "do I need a website?" to "do I like *this* website?"

### Making it (30–60 minutes per prospect)
- Use their **real name, logo (if any), photos** from Facebook/Instagram/Google, and **real reviews** from Google.
- One homepage only: header with name and tagline, a big "Call" or "Book" button, 3 key services or menu highlights, reviews, area covered, opening hours, map.
- Make it **look great on a phone**, because that's where they'll open it.
- Host it on a private, unlisted link (e.g. a staging subdomain). Add a small "Draft concept by [YOUR NAME]" note.
- **Don't** publish it publicly or let it be indexed, and don't present it as their official site. It's a private preview for them only.
- Keep a tidy template per niche so each mockup only takes the time to swap in their content.

> **Photos and reviews:** using their own publicly posted content in a private draft just for them is a common sales approach, but ask permission before using anything on a live site. Never use other people's photos.

### Opening with the mockup (cold call)
> "Hi [OWNER NAME], it's [YOUR NAME], I'm a web designer here in Falmouth. I'll be honest, it's a cold call, but slightly unusual: I've already done something for you. I noticed [BUSINESS] hasn't got a website, so rather than ring you up and *tell* you what one could look like, I put together a rough draft of a homepage. Can I text you the link? It takes 30 seconds to look at, and if you hate it, no hard feelings."

### When they've looked at it
> "So, what did you think? …What would you change? …If that was live tomorrow, where would you put the link, on the van, your Google listing, your Facebook?"

*(Let them design it in their head. When they start suggesting changes, they're already picturing owning it.)*

### Moving from mockup to money
> "Glad you like it. To turn that into a proper working site, with your [extra pages / booking / menu], set up on your own web address and showing up on Google, it's [PRICE] for the [PACKAGE NAME] package. Want me to talk you through what's included?"

### If they say "I'll just use this then"
> "Ha, I wish! It's only a picture of a site at the moment. It's not on your own address, there's nothing behind the buttons, and Google can't see it. That's the bit I do. But I'm glad you like the look of it, because that's the hard part decided."

## 2. Tiered packages

Three tiers works well: people tend to pick the middle one, and the top one makes the middle look reasonable. Keep names plain English.

| | **Starter** | **Standard** (recommended for most) | **Plus** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Sole traders, cleaners, gardeners, groomers, anyone on a tight budget | Most trades, cafés, salons, garages | B&Bs, shops, charters, anyone taking bookings or selling online |
| **Pages** | 1-page site (scrolling) | Up to [5] pages | Up to [10] pages |
| **Includes** | Services, contact, click-to-call, reviews, map, mobile-friendly | Everything in Starter + gallery, separate service pages, contact form, Google Business Profile tidy-up | Everything in Standard + online booking / small shop / gift vouchers / availability calendar |
| **Turnaround** | [1 week] | [2 weeks] | [3–4 weeks] |
| **One-off price** | [STARTER PRICE] | [STANDARD PRICE] | [PLUS PRICE] |
| **Or monthly** | [STARTER MONTHLY] /month | [STANDARD MONTHLY] /month | [PLUS MONTHLY] /month |

*(Fill in what's included to match what you actually deliver. Never promise "page 1 of Google", see guarantees below.)*

### Presenting the tiers on the phone
> "I've got three options. Most people round here go for the middle one. **Starter** is a single page with everything people need to ring you, from [STARTER PRICE]. **Standard** is a proper little site with a few pages, a gallery of your work, and I'll sort out your Google listing too, that's [STANDARD PRICE]. And **Plus** adds online booking [or a shop], that's [PLUS PRICE]. From what you've told me about [THEIR PAIN], I'd say [RECOMMENDED TIER] is right for you, because [REASON]."

### Recommending a tier by niche (rule of thumb)
- **Starter:** cleaners, gardeners, dog groomers, one-person trades just wanting a presence
- **Standard:** most trades, cafés, takeaways, barbers, garages
- **Plus:** B&Bs/holiday lets (direct booking), indie shops (online shop/vouchers), marine charters/trips/courses (online booking), salons with deposits

### Anchoring with their own numbers
Use *their* value, not invented figures:
> "You said a typical [bathroom refit / week's stay / full groom] is about [THEIR FIGURE]. So if the site brings in [one or two] extra of those, it's paid for itself. Does that sound reasonable?"

## 3. Monthly vs one-off

Offer both. Some owners hate big upfront costs; others hate ongoing bills.

### One-off + optional care plan
> "You can pay once, [PRICE], and the site's yours. Then there's an optional care plan at [CARE PRICE] a month, which covers hosting, security updates, backups and up to [X] small changes a month, like new prices or photos. Or you can manage it yourself, I'll show you how."

### Monthly (website as a service)
> "Or if you'd rather not pay a lump sum, it's [MONTHLY PRICE] a month. That covers building it, hosting, updates and changes whenever you need them. Minimum [12] months, then it's rolling, cancel with a month's notice."

**Be upfront about what happens if they cancel monthly.** Do they keep the site, can they buy it out, is the domain theirs? Write it down and say it:
> "The web address is always in your name, so it's yours whatever happens. If you stop the monthly after the first year, you can [take the site with you for BUYOUT PRICE / keep it and host it elsewhere]."

### Helping them choose
> "Honestly? If you'd rather not think about it and just ring me when you need a change, monthly is easier. If you're happy to update the odd thing yourself and like owning it outright, go one-off. Which sounds more like you?"

### Comparing to what they already spend (only if true)
> "You mentioned you pay [Checkatrade / Yell / Booking.com] about [THEIR FIGURE]. This is [MONTHLY PRICE], and it's yours, not a listing next to your competitors."

*(Only use this if they've told you their figure. Don't guess what they pay.)*

## 4. Guarantees (only promise what you control)

Good guarantees remove risk. Bad guarantees ("page one of Google!") are lies waiting to happen. Pick the ones you can stand behind:

**Safe, honest guarantees:**
- **"No-obligation mockup."** "If you don't like the draft, you owe me nothing."
- **"Love it or don't pay."** "You see the finished site before it goes live. If you're not happy after [two] rounds of changes, you don't pay the balance." *(Take a deposit only once they've approved the design direction.)*
- **"On time or [discount]."** "If it's not live by the date we agree, through my fault, you get [X]% off." *(Exclude delays from them sending content late.)*
- **"You own your domain."** "Your web address is registered in your name. You're never held hostage."
- **"Local and reachable."** "If you need something, you ring me, a real person in Falmouth, not a ticket system. I'll reply within [one working day]."
- **"Fixed price."** "The price I quote is the price you pay. No surprise extras unless you ask for something new."

**Never promise:**
- A specific Google ranking ("page one", "top of Google").
- A specific number of new customers or a revenue figure.
- That it'll "pay for itself in a month."

**How to say it:**
> "I can't promise you'll be top of Google, and anyone who does is fibbing. What I can promise is it'll look great, work properly on phones, be set up so Google can find it, and be done by [DATE], or you get money off. And if you don't like the design, you don't pay."

## 5. Urgency without lies

Real deadlines exist in Falmouth and Penryn. Use them honestly. The rule: **every urgency line must be true for this specific business.** If it isn't, don't say it.

### Seasonal deadlines that are genuinely real

**Summer season (Easter to September)**
For cafés, restaurants, B&Bs, holiday lets, shops, marine, tour operators.
> "Visitors start booking and searching from [Easter / spring half-term]. A site takes about [TIMEFRAME] to build, and Google needs a bit of time to pick it up. So if you want it working for this summer, we'd want to start by about [MONTH]. If you'd rather wait, that's fine too, but it might be next summer before it's really paying off."

**Falmouth Week (usually the second week of August)**
A big sailing regatta and festival. Falmouth Week 2026 ran from 7 to 16 August.[^1] Check the dates for next year before using this line. Relevant for B&Bs, holiday lets, cafés, restaurants, takeaways, marine services, boat trips.
> "Falmouth Week's [DATES] next year. The town's rammed and people are searching for somewhere to eat, stay, or get their boat sorted. If you want the site live and ranking by then, starting in [MONTH] gives us plenty of time."

**University term starts (September)**
Falmouth University and the University of Exeter share the Penryn Campus, and Falmouth University also has a campus in town, so thousands of students arrive or return each September. Check the current term dates on the universities' websites before you mention them. Relevant for barbers, hair/beauty, cafés, takeaways, garages (students with old cars), cleaners (student lets), shops.
> "Freshers arrive in [late September]. They don't know anyone, they just search 'barber near me' on their phone. If you're there with prices and a booking button when they arrive, you could pick up regulars for three years. To be ready, we'd need to start by [MONTH]."

**Graduation (typically summer)**
Relevant for B&Bs, restaurants, salons. Families book well in advance.

**Christmas**
Relevant for shops (gift vouchers, online sales), restaurants (Christmas party bookings), salons (gift vouchers, party-season bookings).
> "If you want gift vouchers on there for Christmas, we'd need to start in the next [X] weeks."

**Winter refit / next season (for trades and marine)**
> "Most boat owners plan their winter work in [September/October]. If your lay-up packages are online by then, they'll book you instead of ringing round."

### Your own capacity (only if true)
> "I only take on [X] sites a month so I can do them properly, and I've got space in [MONTH]."

*(Only say this if you genuinely cap your workload. Don't invent "only two slots left.")*

### Time-limited offers (only if real, written down, and honoured)
> "For businesses in Penryn and Falmouth, I'm doing [OFFER, e.g. the first year's hosting free] for anyone who starts before [DATE]."

Then actually end the offer on that date. If you extend it every month, it's not urgency, it's a lie.

### Urgency phrases to never use
- "This price is only for today."
- "I've only got one slot left" (unless literally true).
- "Your competitors are all getting websites" (unless you can name them).
- "You're losing thousands a month" (you don't know that).

## 6. Handling the money conversation: quick talk tracks

**Saying the price** (state it, then stop talking):
> "For the Standard package, it's [STANDARD PRICE]." *(Pause. Let them respond.)*

**Deposit and payment terms:**
> "I take [50]% to start and the rest when you're happy and it goes live. Or if monthly suits better, it's [MONTHLY PRICE] from the start."

**"Can you do it cheaper?"** *(Full objection handling is in a separate file. This is just the pricing angle.)*
> "I'd rather not knock the price down and cut corners. But I could do the Starter version now for [STARTER PRICE], and upgrade it later when you're ready. Would that work?"

**Confirming the deal:**
> "Brilliant. So it's the [PACKAGE] at [PRICE], live by [DATE]. I'll send that over in writing today with what's included. What's the best email? And can you send me [logo / photos / price list] by [DAY]?"

**Always put it in writing:** package, price, payment terms, timeline, what's included, what you need from them, who owns the domain, and what happens if they cancel a monthly plan.
