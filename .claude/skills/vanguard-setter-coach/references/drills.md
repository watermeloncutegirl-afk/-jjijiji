# Drill Scripts

Run every drill one rep at a time. Give the prompt, wait for the setter's answer, grade it in one or two lines, then give the next prompt. End with a summary: score, best rep, worst rep, and one line to say instead.

## Daily: Opener out loud (10 reps)
Pick a scenario for each rep, varying niche and opener type: owner's name known, Google reviews, guarded owner, or on the go.

Grade each rep against these checks:
- [ ] 25 words or fewer before the first stop. That's about 10 seconds at a calm pace. Count the words and report the number.
- [ ] Name and Vanguard in the first sentence
- [ ] Honest framing ("this is a cold call")
- [ ] Permission or a time-bounded ask ("30 seconds… then you tell me")
- [ ] Ends on a statement or ask, then stops; no second sentence trailing on
- [ ] Any bracketed detail is plausibly true. Flag anything unverifiable.
- [ ] Not "How's your day going?", "Did I catch you at a bad time?", or fake familiarity

If the setter is recording audio, remind them to listen back for pace and whether their statements go down at the end. Report a pass rate out of 10.

## Pairs: Smokescreen rapid-fire (10 reps)
Shuffle these 10 and fire them as a gatekeeper. Vary the tone across busy, curt and suspicious.

1. "What's this regarding?"
2. "Is he expecting your call?"
3. "He's busy / with a client / on a job."
4. "Send us an email."
5. "We're not interested."
6. "We already have someone for that."
7. "Can I take a message?"
8. "Who are you with?"
9. "He doesn't take sales calls."
10. "Just tell me and I'll pass it on."

Grade each answer as **ledge → one honest sentence → small ask**, all in one breath:
- 1 point: starts with "Totally fair" or a similar calm acknowledgement where it fits. For direct questions like "Who are you with?", a straight answer counts.
- 1 point: one honest sentence with no pretext
- 1 point: ends on a small ask (a time, an email, or "Is he in?")

The approved answers are in `cheat-sheet.md`. The setter's own wording is fine if it hits all three beats and breaks no rule. A pretext ("he's expecting me") is a hard fail for that rep.

For the advanced version, chain two smokescreens on one call. The setter must then get a callback time or leave a message, not push a third time.

## Pairs: Two-ask price drill (5 rounds)
Each round:
1. You ask: "So how much does this cost?"
   - Pass: acknowledgement, then "it depends", then **one** real fit question. Giving the ballpark straight away is also fine.
2. You answer the fit question, then ask again: "Okay, but roughly what are we talking?"
   - Pass: the approved ballpark ("Most of our builds land around two thousand, one-time, and the demo itself is free."), then a pause written as `[pause]` or "…", then the bridge to the Closer and two options.
   - Fail: a second dodge, any other number, "it's cheap", or a discount.
3. In rounds 3–5, add a follow-up after the ballpark. Rotate through: "too expensive", "Wix is $20", "my nephew", "just email me the price", "what's the catch?" Grade against the approved follow-up lines.

## Weekly: Call review
Ask for three transcripts or detailed notes: one booked, one hang-up, one "send me info." Score each one with `scorecard.md`. For each call, name **the second it turned**, then the pattern across all three. Finish with the one drill for next week and its rep count.

## Bonus drills
- **Timing quiz:** name a niche and a local time, and the setter says "dial" or "wait until…".
- **Fit triage:** read a short prospect profile, and the setter decides book or don't book, and why. For example, "$300 max" means don't book; email the demo instead.
- **Three things exit:** after a role-play gatekeeper call, the setter must recall the owner's name, the gatekeeper's name and the callback time.
