# Building a Grand Slam Offer (BUILD mode)

Ask one question at a time and skip anything the user has already answered.

## Step 0: Pick a starving crowd
Score each item 1–5:
- **Pain:** are they losing money now? Missed calls and no website both cost jobs.
- **Purchasing power:** can they pay $2k+? A towing company can. A side-hustle crafter usually can't.
- **Easy to target:** can you list them? Google Maps, niche directories and associations all work.
- **Growing:** is demand in the niche rising?

Pain matters most. Narrow until the offer can speak their language, for example "auto repair shops in Columbus with no website".

## Step 1: Dream outcome
Prompt: *"If this worked perfectly, what would the owner tell their spouse a month later?"*
- Bad: "a modern responsive website."
- Good: "when people Google us, they find us, trust us and call. I stopped losing jobs to the shop down the road."

## Step 2: List every problem
Walk through the buyer's journey: **before buying, during delivery, after launch**. For each value driver, ask what makes the buyer think it won't work, will take too long, or will take too much of their effort.

Typical agency problems:
- "I've been burned by a freelancer who vanished."
- "I don't have time to write content or take photos."
- "I don't know if a website actually brings customers."
- "It'll take months."
- "I'll have to learn some software to update it."
- "I can't afford a big upfront cost."
- "What if I hate the design?"
- "Who fixes it when it breaks?"

## Step 3: Turn problems into solutions
Reword each problem as "How do I … ?", then name a solution:
- "Burned before" becomes *"You see the finished demo before you pay a cent."*
- "No time for content" becomes *"Done-for-you copy written from a 20-minute call."*
- "Will it bring customers?" becomes *"Click-to-call, Google Business Profile linked, review showcase."*
- "Hate the design" becomes *"Unlimited revisions until launch"* (only if that is actually honoured).

## Step 4: Delivery vehicles
For each solution, choose how it is delivered and estimate what it costs you:

| Vehicle | Value to buyer | Cost to agency |
|---|---|---|
| Done-for-you (DFY) | Highest | Highest |
| Done-with-you (call or workshop) | Medium | Medium |
| DIY (template, video, checklist) | Lower | Near zero once made |
| Software or automation | High and scalable | Build once |
| 1-on-1 or group, live or recorded, response-time SLA | Varies | Varies |

## Step 5: Trim and stack
Plot every solution:
- **High value, low cost:** keep, and feature it prominently.
- **High value, high cost:** keep it in a premium tier, or price it in.
- **Low value, low cost:** use as bonuses.
- **Low value, high cost:** cut.

Stack what remains into a value stack with a defensible standalone value for each line.

## Step 6: Enhance
Use `enhancers.md`: one bonus per remaining objection, a named guarantee, real scarcity or urgency, and a MAGIC name.

## Step 7: Price
Use `pricing.md`: price to value, anchor against the cost of doing nothing, and decide payment options. Payment terms are set by the owner or closer.

## Step 8: Completeness check
Run the 7-point check in `value-equation.md`, list the proof you still need, and write the approved setter lines (the only things setters may say about price or terms).
