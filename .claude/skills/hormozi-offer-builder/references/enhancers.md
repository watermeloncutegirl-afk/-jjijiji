# Enhancers: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees and naming

These multiply demand for a strong core offer. They can't save a weak one. **Every enhancer must be true.**

## Scarcity (limits how many can buy)
- **Honest agency versions:** a real build capacity ("we take 4 new builds a month because one designer handles each one"), or a niche cap ("one towing company per city").
- **Never:** "only 2 spots left" when there's unlimited capacity, or a cap that resets every week.
- **Setter rule:** Vanguard setters never mention spots or limits on the phone. Scarcity is communicated by the closer, and only when it is true.

## Urgency (limits how long they can buy)
- **Honest versions:** a seasonal deadline tied to their business ("live before spring storm season"), a real price increase on a real date, a launch cohort with a fixed start date, or a bonus for buyers who start by a date that is actually enforced.
- **Never:** countdown timers that reset, or "this price is only for today" when it isn't.
- **Cost of delay** is the most honest urgency of all: "every week without a site is calls going to competitors." Use it only if it's plausible for that business.

## Bonuses
Rules:
1. **One bonus per objection.** Each one should answer a specific "yeah, but…".
2. **Name each bonus** and give it a **defensible** standalone value: what you'd actually charge for it alone, or its market price.
3. Choose items that are high value and low cost to deliver: templates, setup work you can systematize, checklists, recorded walkthroughs.
4. Lead with the most valuable bonus.
5. Ideally the bonus stack's total value is greater than the price. Only do this if the values are honest.

Agency bonus ideas, mapped to objections:

| Objection | Bonus |
|---|---|
| "I don't have time" | Done-for-you copy from one 20-minute call |
| "Will it bring customers?" | Google Business Profile tune-up and review-request text template |
| "I'll have to maintain it" | 30 days of free edits after launch |
| "Is it worth it?" | Launch checklist: where to put the link so customers see it |
| "What if I miss calls anyway?" | Missed-call text-back setup (also a natural upsell to automation) |

## Guarantees
Name the guarantee, make it easy to claim, and guarantee only what you control.

| Type | How it works | Agency example | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | Refund, no questions | "Don't love the final site at launch? Full refund." | You have high confidence and a low refund risk |
| **Conditional** | Refund or remedy if *they* did their part and *you* didn't deliver | "Live within 14 days of kick-off, as long as you've sent your logo and approved copy, or your first month of care is free." | Most agencies. It rewards the committed client |
| **Anti-guarantee** | "All sales final", with a stated reason | "Because we build your custom demo before you pay, all sales are final once you approve it." | The proof is so strong up front that a refund isn't the point |
| **Implied** | Performance-linked, or carried by reputation | "Care plan is month-to-month. If we stop earning it, cancel anytime." | Retainers and care plans |

Guarantee **traps** (always flag these):
- Guaranteeing rankings, leads or revenue the agency doesn't control.
- Fine print that makes it impossible to claim.
- A guarantee the owner hasn't signed off on. Setters and closers can't invent one on a call.

## Naming: the MAGIC formula
- **M**agnetic reason why (a season, an event, or "because we already built it")
- **A**vatar (who it's for: "towing companies", "salons")
- **G**oal (the dream outcome)
- **I**ndicate the time frame ("in 14 days")
- **C**ontainer word (System, Launch, Blueprint, Program, Package)

Use only the parts that add clarity, and write 3–5 options to test. Examples:
- "The 14-Day Tow Truck Website Launch"
- "Salon Booking Site, Done-For-You in 14 Days"
- "Never-Miss-A-Call System for HVAC Companies"

A name may be aspirational. It must not promise an outcome that customers won't reach.
