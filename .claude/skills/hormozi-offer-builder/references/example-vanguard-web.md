# Worked example (AUDIT): Vanguard Studios demo-first web offer

## The current offer (as given)
- **Hook:** a free demo website for the business is built *before* the first call. "We already built it. It's done."
- **Core build:** about $2,000, one-time.
- **Bigger build** (booking and gallery): about $2,600.
- **Care plan:** optional, about $200 a month, cancel anytime.
- **Timeline:** live 14 days after the kick-off call.
- **Sales path:** the setter books a 15–30 minute demo call, and the closer shows the demo and quotes an exact price.
- **Target:** local businesses with no website, only a Google listing (salons, trades, auto and towing, restaurants, gyms, daycares).

## Value equation scorecard (prospect's point of view)

| Driver | Score | Evidence | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | 6/10 | "A website" is concrete but feature-shaped | It isn't framed as "customers who Google you can find, trust and call you" |
| Perceived Likelihood | 8/10 | **Their own demo already exists.** That is very strong proof | Little visible niche proof (case studies or reviews from similar businesses) and no named guarantee |
| Time Delay (lower is better) | 3/10 | The demo exists now, and there's a fixed 14-day launch after kick-off | Good. Could add a week-1 quick win |
| Effort & Sacrifice (lower is better) | 4/10 | It's built for them, and the demo is free | It isn't clear who writes copy, sources photos, handles edits or does Google setup |

**Biggest strength:** the pre-built demo. It collapses perceived likelihood and time delay, which is rare and hard for competitors to copy.
**Fix first:** Dream Outcome framing, then make Effort visibly zero.

## 7-point check
1. Value far above price: **Partly.** It depends on framing the outcome.
2. Starving crowd: **Yes.** Businesses with no website are losing searches.
3. Named guarantee: **No.**
4. Three or more named bonuses tied to objections: **No.**
5. Real reason to act now: **Not stated.**
6. Hard to compare: **Yes.** The demo makes it unlike Wix or Fiverr.
7. Name says who and what: **No.**

Score: 2 yes, 1 partial. The core asset is strong, but the packaging leaves value on the table.

## Suggestions for the owner (not current offer terms)
> Everything below is a **suggestion for the owner to approve**. Setters must keep using only the approved lines until the owner changes them.

1. **Reframe the outcome.** "Your Google searchers turn into calls" rather than "a 5-page site".
2. **Add a named, conditional guarantee you control.** For example: *"The 14-Day Launch Promise: live within 14 days of your kick-off call, provided we have your approvals, or your first month of care is on us."* Use this only if the team can actually hit it.
3. **Bonus stack, one per objection, with honest values:**
   - Done-for-you copy from a 20-minute call ("I don't have time")
   - Google Business Profile link-up and review-request text template ("Will it bring customers?")
   - 30 days of post-launch edits ("What if I want changes?")
4. **Tiers:** Launch (about $2,000), Launch Plus with booking and gallery (about $2,600), and Launch Plus with the care plan. Present them as a choice between options rather than a yes or no.
5. **Real urgency only.** For example, seasonal timing ("before your busy season"), or a real build-capacity cap if one exists. If neither is true, don't add urgency.
6. **Proof to collect:** before-and-after screenshots and owner quotes from launched clients, grouped by niche. Don't use them until they exist.
7. **MAGIC name options:**
   - "The 14-Day Local Launch"
   - "Done-For-You Website for [Niche], Live in 14 Days"
   - "Already-Built Website Program"
8. **Natural upsell:** missed-call text-back automation on the care plan (see `example-ai-receptionist.md`).

## What setters may say (unchanged)
- "Most of our builds land around two thousand, one-time, and the demo itself is free."
- "Around twenty-six hundred with booking and a gallery."
- "The build is one-time. There's an optional care plan, about two hundred a month, cancel anytime."
- "Live 14 days after your kick-off call."
- "I can't set prices or terms. That's [Closer]'s call."

Setters never mention discounts, specials, spots or guarantees that the owner hasn't approved.
