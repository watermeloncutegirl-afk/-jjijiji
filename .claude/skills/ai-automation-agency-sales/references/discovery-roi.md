# ROI Discovery Call and Calculator

The ROI number is theirs, not ours. Every input comes from the owner's answers. If they don't know, offer a range and label it "your estimate." Never fill gaps with industry averages unless the user supplies a sourced figure, and say where it came from.

## Call structure (20–30 minutes)

1. **Up-front contract (1 min).** "I'll ask a few questions about how calls and leads come in, show you what this could look like, and if it doesn't make sense we'll say so. Fair?"
2. **Current state (8–10 min).** Ask the calculator questions below, in conversation. Mirror, label, write down their exact words.
3. **Play it back (2 min).** "So you're getting about [X] calls a week, and roughly [Y] go unanswered, mostly when you're on a job. Did I get that right?"
4. **Run the math with them (3 min).** Show the calculator, conservative case first. Ask: "Does that look about right to you?" Adjust to their answer.
5. **Show the demo (5 min).** See `demo-first.md`.
6. **Price and next step (5 min).** See `pricing-proposal.md`. Pilot if they're unsure.

## Questions (map to calculator inputs)

| Input | Question | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A. Inbound calls per week | "Roughly how many calls a week, new customers and existing?" | Ask them to check their phone log if possible |
| B. Share missed / unanswered | "Of those, how many go to voicemail or don't get picked up?" | Get a number or a range; don't suggest one |
| C. Share of missed calls that are new business | "Of the missed ones, how many are new customers vs. existing?" | |
| D. Response time to forms/messages | "When a form or message comes in, how long until someone replies?" | For lead follow-up |
| E. Average job / ticket value | "What's a typical job worth to you?" | First-visit value, not lifetime, unless they want lifetime |
| F. Close rate when you do talk to them | "When you actually get someone on the phone, how many become customers?" | |
| G. Recovery rate (assumption) | Agree together: "If a text went out instantly, how many of those missed people do you think would reply and book? Let's use a low number." | **Always labelled as an assumption** |

## Calculator template

```
ROI ESTIMATE — [Business] — [date]
All inputs provided by [Owner] on our call. Recovery rate is an agreed assumption, not a guarantee.

Inputs
A  Calls per week ................................ [   ]
B  Missed share .................................. [   ]%
C  Missed calls that are new business ............ [   ]%
E  Average job value ............................. $[   ]
F  Close rate when reached ....................... [   ]%
G  Recovery rate (assumption) .................... low [  ]% / mid [  ]% / high [  ]%

Math
Missed new-business calls per month   M = A × B × C × 4.33
Recovered conversations per month     R = M × G
New jobs per month                    J = R × F
Estimated monthly revenue recovered   $ = J × E

                        Conservative     Mid          High
Recovered conversations   [ ]            [ ]          [ ]
New jobs                  [ ]            [ ]          [ ]
Revenue / month           $[ ]           $[ ]         $[ ]

Cost: setup $[ ] + $[ ]/month
Months to cover setup (conservative): setup ÷ (revenue/month − monthly fee) = [ ]
```

Rules:
- Show conservative first and anchor the conversation there.
- If the conservative case doesn't cover the monthly fee, say so and recommend not buying, or a smaller offer. This builds more trust than a stretched number.
- Revenue is not profit. If the owner gives a margin, add a profit row; otherwise note "before costs."
- Round down. Use "about."

For lead follow-up, swap A–C for "form/message leads per month" and "share that never get a reply or reply after [D]". For review requests, don't compute revenue; show "review requests sent per month = customers per month" and let the owner judge the value.

## Questions to qualify the deal
- "Who else would weigh in on this?"
- "If this worked the way we just mapped it, is [price range] something you'd consider?"
- "What would you need to see in a 30-day pilot to keep it?" (write down their success criteria; it goes in the proposal)
