# CLOSER framework for the demo call

CLOSER is Hormozi's sales-call structure (from his $100M sales training and public content). Below it's adapted to a **15–30 minute website demo** where the closer shows a site already built for the prospect and sets the price and terms.

Hormozi's framing: the prospect already wants the result; the closer's job is to help them believe this solution will get it for them, and to make a decision either way. The closer acts like a doctor (diagnose, then prescribe), not a pusher.

## The six steps

### C: Clarify why they're here
Ask why they took the call and let them answer.
- "Jordan mentioned you agreed to take a look at the demo. What made you say yes?"
- "Tell me more about that." Repeat key words back.

### L: Label them with a problem
Restate the problem in your words and confirm it.
- "So right now people find you on Google, but there's no site to land on, and you think some of them just call the next shop. Is that right?"
- Wait for the yes. If it's not right, re-clarify.

### O: Overview past pain
What have they tried, and why didn't it work? This shows you care, tells you what not to pitch, and sets up why this is different.
- "Have you had a website before, or tried Wix, or someone building one?"
- "What happened with that?"
- Common answers: a nephew who never finished it, a freelancer who disappeared, a DIY builder they never had time for, an agency that quoted $8,000.

### S: Sell the vacation (then the plane)
Describe life after the result before explaining how it works. Then show the demo as the "plane".
- "Picture someone searching [service] in [city] at 9pm. They find you, see your work, your reviews, and book or call. How would a few more of those a week change things?"
- Then the demo: walk through it using **their** words from C, L and O. Keep the tech talk short.
- No promised results. Don't say "you'll rank #1" or "you'll get 20 new customers."

### E: Explain away concerns
Ask "What questions do you have for me?", not "What do you think?" or "Are you ready?" Then:
1. **Isolate:** "Other than [concern], is there anything else stopping you?"
2. **Restate as a condition:** "So if we can sort [concern], you'd want to move ahead?"
3. **Answer with proof:** example sites, reviews, a clear process, a written scope, the company's real guarantee (only if one exists).

### R: Reinforce the decision
After the yes, confirm it was a good choice and walk them through the next step right away: payment, kick-off call booked, what happens in the next 14 days. The sale isn't finished until they take the first action. Note in GHL immediately.

## Timing ([EXAMPLE] for a 25-minute demo)

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | C: rapport, why they're here |
| 3–8 | L + O: label the problem, past attempts |
| 8–16 | S: the vacation, then the demo |
| 16–18 | Price and options (per company policy) |
| 18–23 | E: questions and concerns |
| 23–25 | R: payment, kick-off date, next steps |

Rough talk ratio: the prospect's situation gets more of the call than your pitch, especially in the first half. Wrenbjor's Hormozi skill suggests ~70/30 for a 45-min high-ticket call; treat that as a guide.

## The three buying conditions

Hormozi's universal frame: almost every objection means one of three things is missing.

1. **They believe it will work** for them → more proof, show the mechanism, case examples.
2. **They trust you** → slow down, listen more, be candid about limits.
3. **They can afford it** → scope, payment options the company actually offers, priorities.

Diagnose which condition is missing rather than arguing the stated objection: "Let's figure this out together. Is it more about whether it'll work, about us, or about the money?"

## Objection loops (honest versions)

**Price: "It's too much."**
- "It's not a small number. Is it the total, or the timing of paying it?"
- Cost of inaction (only with their own numbers): "You said a new client is worth about $X to you. How many would it take to cover it?"
- Offer only real options (a smaller scope, the payment plan if the company has one). **Don't make up discounts.** Hormozi's general advice is to add value instead of cutting price; only offer a bonus that's real company policy, never a fake "today only".

**Spouse / partner: "I need to talk to my wife/husband/partner."**
- "Makes sense. What do you think they'll say?"
- If positive: "Want to grab them for two minutes now, or set a quick three-way call?"
- If unsure: "What part do you think they'd worry about?" Often it's the prospect's own doubt.

**Time: "I need to think about it."**
- "Totally fair. What specifically do you want to think through: whether it'll work, the price, or the timing?"
- Address that one thing. Then: "If you decide later, when would be the right time, and what would change?"
- If it's still no, set a specific follow-up date. Don't pressure.

**Trust: "How do I know this works?"**
- Acknowledge past bad experiences (the freelancer who vanished).
- Show real client sites and real reviews. Explain the process and what's in writing.
- If the company has a guarantee, explain it exactly as written. Never improvise one.

**"Wix is $20 a month" / "My nephew does websites"**
- Never trash the alternative. "For some businesses that's right. The difference is who does the work and how fast it's live."

**Closing tactics:** silence after the closing question is fine. Don't use fake deadlines, invented scarcity ("two spots left") or anything you'd be uncomfortable seeing written in a review.

## Closer scorecard (0/1/2 each, 14 max)

| Step | What "2" looks like |
|---|---|
| Clarify | Prospect stated their own reason in their own words |
| Label | Problem restated and the prospect confirmed it |
| Overview | Past attempts surfaced and used later in the pitch |
| Sell the vacation | Outcome painted in their terms before the demo; no promised results |
| Explain concerns | Asked "what questions do you have?", isolated, answered with proof |
| Reinforce | Payment + kick-off booked on the call, next steps clear |
| Honesty & policy | Price/terms within policy, no invented urgency or claims (a violation is an auto-fail) |
