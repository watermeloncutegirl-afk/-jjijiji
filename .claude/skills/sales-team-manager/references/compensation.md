# Compensation design

All dollar figures in this file are **[EXAMPLE]** values. Replace them with the user's real numbers, margins and local wage laws.

## Principles

1. **Base + variable.** Hormozi's workshop recommends roughly 50/50: base covers basic needs, variable ties lifestyle to performance. Pure commission makes reps unstable and desperate (which shows up as pushiness). Pure salary removes drive. Predictable Revenue suggests 60/40 or 70/30 for SDRs. Pick a split and explain why.
2. **Commission on cash collected**, not booked revenue. Hormozi calls this the change that most reliably improves deal quality and brings cash forward. A deal that gets refunded or never pays earns nothing.
3. **Pay setters for qualified shows, not bookings.** Paying per booking rewards junk appointments. Pay when a qualified prospect actually attends, plus a share when it closes so setters care about quality.
4. **Simple enough to calculate in your head.** If a rep can't work out what a call earned them, the plan won't change behavior.
5. **Culture over comp.** If the comp plan is the only thing driving behavior, the culture is broken (Hormozi). Comp supports the system; it doesn't replace management.
6. **Write it down.** Put the plan in a signed document: what counts as a qualified show, when commission is earned, what happens with refunds/chargebacks, payout dates. Some US states (e.g. California) require written commission agreements.
7. **Legal floor.** Hourly employees must get at least the applicable minimum wage for all hours worked, including training. Check local law before treating reps as contractors. Recommend a lawyer or accountant review.

## Definitions to agree before launch

- **Qualified show:** the decision-maker attends the demo, has a business that fits, and didn't say "$300 max" on the setting call. The closer marks it in GHL within 24h; disputes go to the manager.
- **Cash collected:** money actually received. If the build is paid in installments, commission is paid on each installment as it arrives.
- **Clawback:** if a deal is refunded within [X] days, the commission on the refunded amount is deducted from the next payout.
- **Payout schedule:** e.g. base every two weeks; variable monthly for the previous month's cash collected.

## Template A: setter ([EXAMPLE])

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base | $[16]/hour (≥ local minimum wage) |
| Per qualified show | $[30] |
| Per closed deal (cash collected) | $[100] per build, or [5]% of the first payment |
| Monthly kicker | $[300] if ≥ [40] qualified shows and zero compliance issues |

**Worked example, one month:**
- 160 hours × $16 = $2,560 base
- 40 qualified shows × $30 = $1,200
- 10 closes × $100 = $1,000
- Kicker hit = $300
- **Total: $5,060** (≈ 51% base / 49% variable)

Variable setter cost per close this month: ($1,200 + $1,000 + $300) ÷ 10 closes = $250 per deal, plus the base. Check that against the gross margin on a ~$2,000 build.

## Template B: closer ([EXAMPLE])

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base | $[3,500]/month |
| Commission | [10]% of cash collected on builds |
| Care plan | [1] month's fee on each care plan sold, paid after the 2nd payment clears |
| Quality gate | Commission rate drops to [7]% for any month with refund rate above [X]% |

**Worked example, one month:**
- 60 qualified shows × 30% close rate = 18 builds
- 18 × average $2,100 cash collected = $37,800 → 10% = $3,780
- 8 care plans × $200 = $1,600
- Base $3,500
- **Total: $8,880** (≈ 39% base / 61% variable)

Adjust until the split is near what you chose (e.g. 50/50 at target performance).

## Template C: founder-led early team (simplest)

When there are only 1–2 setters and the founder closes:
- Hourly base + $[X] per qualified show + $[Y] per close. Review after 60 days with real data.

## Common mistakes

- Paying per booked appointment → calendars full of no-shows and unqualified owners.
- Paying the closer on "contract value" when clients pay late or not at all.
- Changing the plan mid-month without notice. Announce changes 30 days ahead and grandfather the current month.
- Uncapped spiffs and fake contests that encourage pressure tactics. Contests are fine if they reward honest, measurable behavior (e.g. most qualified shows with zero compliance flags).
- Setter commission with no tie to show quality.
