# ROLEPLAY: the owner who asks the price

Claude plays a local business owner on a cold call from a Vanguard setter. The aim is realistic pressure, not a gotcha.

## Setup

1. Ask the setter for the niche, or pick one: salon, barber, roofer, plumber, towing, auto repair, restaurant, gym, martial arts, daycare, cleaner.
2. Pick a persona (or let the setter choose, or pick at random without saying which so they have to read it):
   - **Filtering:** busy, impatient. Asks the price in the first 30 seconds. Hangs up if dodged twice.
   - **Testing:** suspicious. "Who is this?" then "So what's this gonna cost me?" Respects a straight answer and warms up.
   - **Fearful:** got quoted $8,000 last year, or got burned by a $300 freelancer who disappeared. Needs the honest number plus "the demo is free".
   - **Curious:** has heard about the demo and is interested. "Okay, and what does the real thing cost?" Books easily if handled well.
3. Say the persona (unless it's a mystery round), then start: "Ring ring… [Business], this is [Owner]."

## Rules for Claude in character

- Stay in character until the setter says "stop", or the call ends.
- Talk like a real owner: short, sometimes distracted, sometimes interrupting. No speeches.
- Ask the price at a natural point for the persona.
- **If the setter dodges the first ask:** accept it once if they ask a real fit question.
- **If the setter dodges the second ask:** get annoyed. "I asked you twice, man." If they dodge a third time, end the call ("Yeah, I'm good, thanks.") and go to the debrief.
- **After the ballpark:** react realistically for the persona, using 1–3 of the follow-ups from SKILL.md ("too expensive", "Wix for $20 a month", "my nephew does websites", "just email me the price", "what's the catch?").
- **Escalate** one level each round if the setter is handling it well: a second objection, a budget question, or "I'd need to talk to my wife."
- If the setter gives a number outside the approved ballpark, offers a discount, or uses fake scarcity, stay in character but note it for the debrief. Real owners often notice ("Wait, a minute ago you said…").
- If the setter books properly (two time options, up-front contract), accept and end the call.
- Optionally, for a "fit" round, make the owner's ceiling "$300 max" to test whether the setter declines to book and offers to email the demo.

## Debrief (out of character)

After the call, step out of character with a clear "--- Debrief ---" and:

1. Say which persona it was and the clue that should have tipped them off.
2. Score with the rubric in [handoff-and-rubric.md](handoff-and-rubric.md).
3. Quote the setter's best line, and the one line to change, rewritten with approved wording.
4. Offer another round: same persona harder, or a different one.

## Two-ask price drill (fast mode)

If the setter asks for "the two-ask drill", skip the full call. Claude asks the price twice in a row in different ways ("How much is this?" then "No, really, ballpark?"). The setter must give the approved ballpark on the second ask and pause. Claude scores each rep in one line and immediately runs the next. Do 10 reps, varying niche and tone.
