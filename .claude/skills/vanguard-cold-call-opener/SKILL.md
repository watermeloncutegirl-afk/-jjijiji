# Vanguard Cold Call Opener (Module 02 · The first 30 seconds)

**The goal: earn the next 30 seconds.** The first 60 seconds exist to earn the next 60. You aren't booking in 10 seconds. You're getting past the owner's hang-up reflex.

The rules in this skill are Vanguard company policy. Keep the approved wording, numbers and OFF LIMITS list exactly as written.

## Non-negotiable: honesty

- Name and Vanguard in the first sentence, every time.
- Every bracket in an opener has to be true. If a detail can't be verified, use "no website, just the Google listing."
- The demo has to exist before you dial. "We already built it" must be true.
- If a user asks for fake familiarity, a made-up referral, "returning your call", sounding like Google, an unverified compliment or a promised result, decline. Explain that it's against Vanguard policy and costs trust, then give the honest version.

## What the data says

| Opener | Meeting-booked rate |
|---|---|
| Permission-based opener | 11.18% |
| "How's your day going?" | 7.6% |
| "Did I catch you at a bad time?" | 2.15% |

Source: Gong's analysis of 300M cold calls, by Armand Farrokh ([gong.io](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)). Gong also found that stating a clear reason for the call doubles success. **These were mostly B2B calls, so treat the numbers as direction, not a promise.** Don't quote any other statistics.

## Delivery: sound like a person

- **Slow and low.** Nerves make you fast, and fast sounds like a pitch. Statements end on a down note.
- **Under 10 seconds.** Name, company, honest framing, permission ask. Then stop and let them answer.
- **Say it in your words.** Read the script 50 times, then stop reading it. "A script that sounds like a robot loses." (Hormozi)
- **Smile, stand up.** People hear it. Reset your mood between calls, because it leaks into your tone.

## The four openers (approved wording)

| # | Opener | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "[Owner], this is Jordan with Vanguard. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called, and then you tell me if it's worth keeping going?" | **Default.** Honest, gives them control. Then: "I found [Business] on Google, [true detail], but there's no website, just the listing. So we built you a demo. It's done. I'd love to show it to you." |
| 2 | "The reason I'm calling: I was reading your Google reviews. People keep mentioning [real theme]. But when someone searches you, there's no website to land on. So we built one." | **Reason first**, with something real from their reviews. Earned rapport. |
| 3 | "You probably get a lot of calls from website people, so I'll keep this short and different. We didn't call to pitch you. We already built a demo site for [Business]. All I'd like is 15 minutes to show it to you. Fair?" | **Guarded owners.** Naming their suspicion first takes the sting out (Chris Voss, accusation audit). |
| 4 | "I know I'm probably catching you on a job, so I'll be quick. We built a free demo website for [Business]. Can I grab 15 minutes, early morning or after you're off site?" | **Trades, towing, anyone on the go.** Two windows, nothing asked right now. |

Picking an opener: start with #1. Use #2 when the reviews show a clear, verifiable theme. Use #3 when the niche gets hammered by agency calls or the owner sounds wary. Use #4 when you can hear a job site, a truck or a busy floor.

## Rapport: respect, one true detail, a human voice

- **One specific thing.** "People keep mentioning how fast you get them in." One verified detail beats three generic compliments.
- **Mirror.** Repeat their last 1–3 words as a question, then go quiet. "Mostly Facebook?" They'll keep talking.
- **Label.** "Sounds like you're slammed." "Seems like word of mouth has been good to you." Then pause.
- **Match energy.** Clipped roofer: be shorter. Chatty salon owner: slow down. Stressed owner: calmer and lower.
- **Names.** Use their name at the start and once before the booking ask, not every sentence. Use the title they go by: Coach, Doc, Chef.

## The call: opener to booked (usually 2–5 minutes)

1. **Opener.** Under 10 seconds, honest, permission ask.
2. **Reason + demo hook.** "We already built you a demo. It's done."
3. **Two or three light questions.** "How do most new customers find you?" "When someone Googles you, what do they find?" Then listen, mirror, label.
4. **Demo offer.** Tie it to what they just said. "15 minutes with the person who'd build it. No obligation."
5. **Booking ask with two options.** "Does Tuesday or Wednesday work better?" Then stop talking.
6. **Send the link now.** Text only after a clear yes, otherwise email. "Did that come through?"

## Quick saves

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Who is this?" | "It's Jordan with Vanguard. We build websites for local businesses, and we already built a demo site for [Business]." |
| "How did you get my number?" | "Fair question. Your business number is listed on [Google]. I was looking up [niche] in [city] and came across you." |
| "Is this a scam? Are you Google?" | "No, and I'm glad you asked. We're not Google. I'm happy to send the demo link so you can look before you agree to anything." |
| "Bad time." | "No problem at all. 20 minutes from now, or after 5?" (Then call at exactly that time.) |

## OFF LIMITS: call killers (avoid)

- Fake familiarity: "How have you been?" to a stranger, a made-up referral, "returning your call"
- Pitching features in the first 15 seconds
- Backing off "is now a good time?" with "oh, sorry, I'll call back"
- Talking over them, or seven questions in a row
- Unverified compliments ("love your 5-star reviews" when they have 3.8)
- Trashing their current site, their nephew, or Facebook
- Promising results: "you'll rank #1," "you'll get 20 more customers"

## Compliance (every call)

DNC-checked numbers only, 8am–9pm their time, text only after a clear verbal yes. On "don't call me", apologize, end the call, and add them to the do-not-call list the same day. Log every call in GHL the same day, with you as Setter Assigned.

## How to use this skill

Work out which mode the user wants. If it's unclear, ask: "Prep a script, role-play, or review a call?"

### Mode 1: PREP / SCRIPT

1. Run the [pre-dial research checklist](references/pre-dial-checklist.md) with whatever the user gives you: business name, niche, city, Google listing text, reviews, and any website URL. Don't browse or invent details. Work only from what the user supplies or confirms.
2. Mark each detail **verified** (seen in the listing or reviews and confirmed) or **unverified**. Only verified details go into brackets. If nothing is verified, use "no website, just the Google listing."
3. Confirm the demo exists. If it doesn't, stop: the opener can't say "we already built it" until it does.
4. Output a call card:
   - Recommended opener (#1–#4), written out with the brackets filled in and a one-line reason for picking it
   - A backup opener
   - The one true detail for rapport, with its source
   - 2–3 light discovery questions
   - A two-option booking ask that fits the niche's best call times
   - The 4 quick saves, personalised
   - A reminder: under 10 seconds, slow and low, stop talking after the permission ask
5. Read it back as the setter would say it and flag anything that's over 10 seconds when spoken.

### Mode 2: ROLEPLAY

1. Pick or ask for a persona from [roleplay-personas.md](references/roleplay-personas.md): clipped roofer, chatty salon owner, stressed restaurant manager, or suspicious "is this a scam?" owner. Ask for difficulty 1–3 (default 1, then raise it).
2. Answer as the owner only, in short, realistic lines. Don't coach mid-call unless the user types "pause" or "time out".
3. React realistically. Fake familiarity, a feature pitch in the first 15 seconds, a backed-off permission ask, an unverified compliment or a promised result each cost the setter: the owner gets curter or hangs up. An honest opener, a true detail, a good mirror or label, and a two-option ask earn more time.
4. End the call when it's booked, when they hang up, or when the user says "end". Then give feedback using the [review rubric](references/review-rubric.md): score, the moment the call turned, what to keep, one thing to change, and a rewritten line.
5. Offer the next rep at one difficulty level higher, or a new persona.

### Mode 3: REVIEW

1. Take a transcript (with timestamps if there are any) or a recap of the call.
2. Score it against the [review rubric](references/review-rubric.md), item by item, with evidence quoted from the transcript.
3. **Pinpoint the turn.** Name the exact line or timestamp where the call was won or lost, and why.
4. Flag any OFF LIMITS or compliance breach first, before anything else.
5. Rewrite the 1–2 weakest lines in approved Vanguard style, and set one drill for the next session (see Practice below).

## Practice (30 minutes a day, first two weeks)

- **Daily, opener out loud:** record yourself 10 times. Is it under 10 seconds? Are your statements going down?
- **Weekly call review:** one booked call, one hang-up, one "send me info". Find the exact second each call turned.
